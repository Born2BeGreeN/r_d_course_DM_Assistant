The Passage of Doom
While traversing tunnels on the Darklake the party 
encounters threats by land, sea, and air.
Your Darklake voyage takes you into a narrow, thirty- 
foot wide, Faerzeress lit tunnel of eerie quiet. The 
sound of the paddles gently lapping on the water 
echoes on the walls, while water occasionally drips 
from unseen sources high above. The eerie purple glow
illuminates blind cave fish that move mindlessly about 
near the surface of the water. Some of the fish are so 
unaware of your presence that they run into your boat.
As you round a bend you see a diamond shaped small 
islet of limestone and stalagmites fifty feet ahead. The 
tunnel forks at the islet, with the Darklake flowing to 
the left or right of the forty foot wide bit of dry rock.
Your eyes are drawn to a wicked sight upon the islet – 
a pyramid of bones, at least eight feet high and five 
feet wide at its base.  Sitting atop this pile is a large 
horned ram's skull, carefully positioned to be facing 
your direction, its empty sockets gazing intently at you. 
As you consider the significance of this display - 
ZZZZAP!
Some bizarre, violent beam of light from behind a 
stalactite above the islet just put a hole in your keel 
boat! You're taking on water, fast, but you don't have 
time to think. A ten foot diameter floating sphere has 
emerged from behind the large stalactite and is 
floating your way.
Roll for initiative!  
This Faerzeress infused area is heavily tainted by 
the influence of Orcus. As a result any creature 
that dies in the area and is not laid to rest 
properly is raised as a zombie or skeleton within 1
hour. Furthermore, undead in the area have 
advantage vs being turned. 
The Zombie Beholder
The Orcus tainted Faerzeress has attracted a 
Zombie Beholder, and the taint has enhanced the
beast somewhat. It is not entirely mindless 
anymore - it has an intelligence score of 8 (-1), 
rather than 3.  Additionally it can use two rays per
round, with one always being its disintegration ray
that it enjoys aiming at boats. 
Only a few days ago the Zombie Beholder was 
drawn to the taint of negative energy in the area 
and moved in, disintegrating a home for itself in 
the large stalactite above the islet. It now serves 
the taint, seeking victims to slay and increasing 
the numbers of undead in the area. Its preferred 
method of slaughter is drowning and it uses its 
disintegration ray to sink the boats of travelers 
who come near. This tunnel is well traveled so it 
has already found several victims. As a result the 
beast is not alone.
Zombies in the water
The Darklake is about twenty-feet deep in these 
passages. Shuffling about at the bottom of the 
Darklake in this area are two boat loads of 
victims. Two keel boats of Duegar and Derro fell 
victim to the beholder recently, and as a result 
eight Duergar zombies and five Derro zombies 
(Use standard zombie stats) wander the bottom of
the Darklake here. They shuffle mindlessly about, 
gnawing at cave fish, waiting for other boats to 
sink. Should any character sink to the bottom 
they will be attacked by 1d3 per round until all 
thirteen zombies swarm them.
Disintegration, Sinking, Swimming and 
Underwater Combat. 
Keel Boats (pg 119 DMG) have 100 hit points. The Zombie 
Beholder's disintegration ray averages 45 hit points of damage. 
Large or smaller inanimate objects are instantly disintegrated 
per the rules, which puts the PC's in the water on the first shot. 
However, rather than instant destruction I suggest the first ray 
puts a hole in the boat, giving it 10 rounds to sink. On the 
second hit the boat starts breaking up, accelerating its sinking 
by 5 rounds. On a third hit the boat is destroyed. This provides 
some sinking boat suspense and gives the player's a chance to 
attempt to scramble to repair the boat, plugging the holes 
through physical means or a mending spell.  How well those 
efforts work is up to the DM.
Swimming (PHB 182) is considered difficult terrain unless a 
creature has a swim speed. 
Use Suffocation rules for drowning-

When a creature runs out of breath, it can survive a number of

Underwater Combat (PHB 198) When making a melee weapon 
attack, a creature that doesn't have a swimming speed has 
disadvantage on the attack roll unless the weapon is a dagger, 
javelin, shortsword, spear or trident.
A ranged weapon attack automatically misses a target beyond the
weapon's normal range. Even against a target within normal range, 
the attack roll has disadvantage unless the weapon is a crossbow, a
net, or a thrown weapon like a javelin (including a spear, trident, or 
dart).
Creatures and objects that are fully immersed in water have 
resistance to fire damage.
The Islet and the Shrine
The islet is roughly diamond shaped, forty feet 
wide and sixty feet deep. In its center is a large 
stalagmite, 25 feet in diameter, that reaches up 80
feet. High above that stalagmite is a huge 60-foot 
thick stalactite. The Zombie Beholder has carved 
a rough cavern lair for itself in this stalactite.
The pyramid of bones is a bizarrely spontaneous 
creation, a shrine to Orcus that gathered itself 
together out of nothingness. The rams head skull 
that sits atop it does not belong to any underdark 
species. 
Should any intelligent living creature set foot on 
the islet the bones burst forth and attack. They 
immediately form ten skeletons and one rams 
headed Minotaur Skeleton. If zombies remain in 
the water they will be attracted to the fight on 
land as well. 1D3 will emerge per round from 
various directions until all thirteen are engaged.
Treasure
The Duergar had some valuables in their boat. At 
the bottom of the water twenty feet off the islet to 
the right can be found a small chest containing 
120 silver, 30 gold, four spell scrolls (three wizard-
Poison Spray, Burning Hands, Mirror Image and 
one cleric – inflict wounds) and one Driftglobe. 
If boats were lost then a functional keel boat, or 
two, may have conveniently washed ashore on the 
back side of the island, their owners no doubt 
having fallen victim to some peril.
Finally -If your player's had to lose armor to avoid 
drowning you could provide them with 
replacement suits on the islet, of lesser, equal, or 
greater utility, as you see fit.
Should the PCs manage to enter the Beholder's 
carved lair in the huge stalactite high above, it is 
nothing but a big disintegration carved hole.
Changing Difficulty
For a lower level party reduce the number of zombies and 
skeletons and eliminate the Zombie Beholder's 
enhancements. 
For a higher level party change the drowned victims to ghouls 
or ghasts, give the Beholder use of more eye rays, and perhaps 
add another Zombie Beholder!
The difficulty of the encounter can also be adjusted by having 
the party attacked closer or farther from shore, and by adjusting
the effectiveness of disintegration to instant destruction. 
Nothing ramps up peril quite like an entire party swimming 
through undead infested waters.
