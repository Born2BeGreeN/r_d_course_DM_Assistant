All Aboard the Golden Mast 
 
 
A Swashbuckling Adventure Beginning in Vilhon Reach of the 
Forgotten Realms 
 
 
Introduction:  A group of adventurers have all chanced upon tiny bottles with lengths of cloth stuffed 
into them. They appear to be strips torn from a larger tapestry, or perhaps fancy bed clothes. There’s 
an identical, handwritten message on each that reads as follows: “Fortune & fame await! Meet me 
by the southern dock of Shulth before first light on the third day past fullest moon, and be ready to 
set sail! You’ll know me by my fine scarlet coat. - Lord Dunstuhl.” 
 
 
a 4(ish)-hour adventure for 1st-3rd level characters 
 
 
by Bryan T. Livengood

All Aboard the Golden Mast 
 
Never will ye find a less hospitable place, but never 
will ye have more fun. 
   —Proverb of the Pirate Isles 
Area Information 
This adventure begins in the nondescript port town of 
Shulth, not far off the Emerald Way along the south-
eastern waters of The Vilhon Reach, near where it 
meets the Sea of Fallen Stars . It’s mostly a pitstop for 
small to medium vessels on their way in and out of the 
larger sea toward The Vilhon Reach’s many other ports. 
The entire town resides along the water and includes a 
small general store, a few knickknack vendors, a thriving 
fishery, sporadic residential dwellings, and a large wharf 
area with a combination inn, tavern, and restaurant 
called The Shattered Oar . There’s also a long dock that 
can accommodate 8-10 ships, depending on size – there 
are currently four small ships, plus one medium-sized 
ship that just happens to be docked in the southern-
most position. 
 
Party Arrival 
The adventurers could find themselves in Shulth for a 
myriad of reasons:  they’re from nearby and looking for 
an escape at sea; they wandered there from The 
Emerald Road; they came by boat, but couldn’t afford 
further passage; they have a Stowaway background 
(see below); etc. 
 
Whatever their reason for being there, they’ve all 
happened upon the same message in a bottle, and 
have decided to heed the call for “fortune and fame.” 
[DM Note:  Allow your players the opportunity to 
purchase any extra generic supplies they may 
want/need before the adventure begins because once 
they meet Lord Dunstuhl there’s no turning back 
(unless, of course, they decide to turn back, but I don’t 
expect them to).] 
 
Lord Dunstuhl 
Lord Octavius Overdale Merriweather Dunstuhl  is a 
slight, garish human that swears he has ancient high elf 
in his “Overdale bloodline.” He wears black pants – 
stuffed into shiny, brass-buckled, mid-calf black boots – 
that are cinched at the waist with a delicate, yellow 
rope that he claims was once the curtain pull in an 
elven castle. To this, he adds a ruffled, open-chested 
(to show off his many bejeweled necklaces) white shirt 
that he tucks into his pants. Topping it all off, he sports 
a double-breasted, brilliantly scarlet coat with many 
yellow accents that almost match his rope belt. The 
coat is obviously too big for him; his hands often 
disappear into its sleeves, and it nearly drags the 
ground as he walks. 
 
Very little is known about Lord Dunstuhl around Shulth 
except for what he’s told people (probably several 
times over). Aside from his tales of elvish heritage, the 
main tales one is likely to hear pertain to his previous 
career as a dread pirate on the Sea of Fallen Stars. 
According to the legend from his own mouth, any ship 
that merely thought they glimpsed the scarlet banners 
on his fabled Golden Mast would take to oar and 
navigate away from him with speed. The story of how 
he ended up adrift on land with no treasure, crew, or 
ship, however, varies: 
 
Roll 1d6 to Determine Dunstuhl’s Story 
1 – He was made to walk the plank in a mutiny, but survived 
due to his tremendous skill and moxie. 
2 – He gave up the pirate’s life for a woman who eventually 
scorned him and took everything. 
3 – Several pirate crews ganged up and attacked his ship 
while they slept – he was the sole survivor. 
4 – He surrendered his ship and all he had to one of the 
Overdale high elves. 
5 – Dragons! 
6 – Everything still exists, hidden away, waiting for him to 
come get it.

The Adventure Begins 
The adventurers arrive at the southern dock of Shulth 
just as the mysterious message stated. There they see 
Lord Dunstuhl, alone, standing nervously next to a 
modest-sized ship moored to the last berth. Lord 
Dunstuhl stops wringing his hands and beams as he 
notices the party. 
“Greetings, travelers, and, well, my faithful new crew! I 
am Lord Octavius Overdale Merriweather Dunstuhl . I 
take it you’ve noticed my fine scarlet coat?” He pulls at 
the breasts of his coat in a showy gesture. “Quick 
introductions are in order, but then we must make haste 
to this terrific vessel, The Haberdasher, if we’re to be 
full sail by sunrise! So, who wants to go first?” 
Here the party will make quick introductions and Lord 
Dunstuhl will interact with them joyfully. Once this is 
complete, he’ll usher everyone quickly onto the 
Haberdasher ahead of him while he unmoors the ship, 
and the players will instantly roll for initiative. 
 
The Haberdasher 
As the heroes step aboard The Haberdasher ahead of 
Lord Dunstuhl, two lizardfolk emerge from below 
decks. Lord Dunstuhl yells something about heathen 
bandits, drawing their ire, and they attack. The ship is 
fairly small, so the combat will be tight. Lord Dunstuhl 
will remain on the dock throughout all combat rounds. 
[DM Note:  If a player attempts to toss someone 
overboard, have the victim roll a STR save to see if the 
player is successful. If someone is knocked off, assume 
they will re-board the ship and re-enter combat after 3 
rounds (unless a player rolls a natural 20 and you 
decide whoever gets knocked overboard is eaten by 
sharks, decides to bail, or something else).] 
 
There are two more standard lizardfolk left below 
decks, as well as their leader, Straaz. If combat is going 
too well, or the adventurers do something that would 
alert them aside from standard combat actions and 
spells, have one or both of those standard lizardfolk go 
topside; otherwise, leave them below with their leader 
for a future encounter. Once the combat above decks 
is finished, Lord Dunstuhl will come aboard and say 
he’ll go about readying the ship to leave port while the 
adventurers check the rest of the ship. In theory, they’ll 
then encounter Straaz and whatever guard he has left; 
however, they may choose to stay up top and help 
Lord Dunstuhl. If so, don’t forget that Straaz & Co. are 
below and will likely come see what’s going on when 
they notice they’re moving. [DM Note:  If you are 
running this module with lower-level characters, use 
your discretion as to whether or not to omit the two 
lizardfolk crew below decks with Straaz as such an 
encounter may be too difficult for adventurers below 
level three.] 
 
After all the lizardfolk and their leader are defeated, 
the adventurers may choose to investigate the ship. If 
they do this before Lord Dunstuhl has a chance to go 
below decks, and succeed on a DC 8 Perception check 
when searching, they’ll find paperwork stating that The 
Haberdasher was, in fact, Straaz’s ship. Lord Dunstuhl 
has used the party of adventurers to steal it for him. 
Without this information, the party will believe Lord 
Dunstuhl’s lie that it’s his ship and the lizardfolk were 
merely bandits attempting to steal it from him since 
they likely knew he previously had no crew to protect 
it. [DM Note:  There’s also a possibility that the 
encounter with Straaz will come to a non-violent 
conclusion. If that’s the case, he becomes an NPC that’s 
willing to help the party find the treasure in question, 
but he will absolutely, under no circumstances 
whatsoever, even with a natural 20, work with 
Dunstuhl. This situation becomes mostly up to you to 
riff and see how the story moves forward.] 
 
The Vilhon Reach 
At this point, regardless of the outcome and what the 
adventurers know, the ship should be well into The 
Vilhon Reach as Lord Dunstuhl’s top priority would 
have been to get the ship free and away from Shulth as 
soon as possible. 
 
If the party knows of Lord Dunstuhl’s treachery, they 
may decide to kill him. Lord Dunstuhl will protest 
greatly, saying this was all for the sake of treasure. He’ll 
say he needed to deceive the party because otherwise 
no one would believe him, or they would just try to 
steal his map and find the treasure themselves (FYI:  
the messages in the bottles were written on strips torn 
from the edges of the treasure map). If the party 
decides to kill him anyway, it wouldn’t involve combat 
as Lord Dunstuhl is weak and unarmed. When looted, 
Lord Dunstuhl will yield 12 gp, jewelry worth roughly 
10 gp, the treasure map, a small vial of red liquid, and a 
conch shell. Without Lord Dunstuhl, the party will have 
to make checks to both sail (DC 11 DEX without an 
applicable skill) and navigate (DC 11 INT without an

applicable skill) the ship, and may likely get off course, 
taking extra time and eating into their rations (unless 
Straaz is with them, then his sailing expertise can get 
them where they’re going posthaste). [DM Note:  If 
they happen to toss Lord Dunstuhl overboard before 
they get the map, have it come floating to the surface 
and have them come up with some way to get it, using 
applicable checks for things like swimming, throwing 
nets, etc. They would not be able to loot the other 
items, however.] 
 
If the confrontation with Lord Dunstuhl is non-lethal, or 
the party doesn’t know they were duped, Lord 
Dunstuhl will divulge his plan: 
 
“The Pirate Isles are a treasure trove, but also a 
terribly dangerous, unsavory place to find oneself. 
Luckily, I now have you fearsome adventurers at my 
side – the fortunes we will collect from this will be 
immeasurable!” He holds up the map triumphantly. 
“Only one thing stands in our way:  Grandeur Kaine. He 
and his men block the path to our fortune as it lies 
beyond their waters in the Sea of Fallen Stars. My plan 
is to catch a great creature from the deeps and then 
unleash it upon Kaine, securing our passage through 
his domain while he’s busy with the beast!” 
 
Lord Dunstuhl goes on to explain how he has acquired 
a magical conch from a group of Kraken Priests that, 
when played, can subdue even the most savage beast 
of the sea. Lord Dunstuhl knows where to find such a 
creature, but it will be up to the party to figure out 
how to lure it out of the depths so the conch can be 
used. [DM Note:  If Lord Dunstuhl is gone, and the 
conch went with him, the party will (most likely) make 
their way directly to the treasure location on the map, 
thus having to fight off Grandeur Kaine directly. If this 
is the case, you can skip over the Sea of Fallen Stars 
section and go directly to The Pirate Isles section. If 
Lord Dunstuhl is gone, but the conch remains, a party 
member may discover its magical function by 
succeeding on a DC 15 Insight or DC 12 Arcana check 
(the DCs drop by five if a party member decides to cast 
Detect Magic on the conch first). Even with this 
information, however, the party will have to determine 
what, if anything, they can do with the conch without 
Dunstuhl to guide them. This scenario can play out to 
your discretion, though the conch will only work at the 
red star as detailed in the following section.] 
 
Sea of Fallen Stars 
Once the ship spills out of the Vilhon Reach, the 
adventurers will find themselves in the Sea of Fallen 
Stars. At this point, Lord Dunstuhl will show them the 
treasure map, and indicate that they are headed for 
the red star that originally marked a spot to avoid 
because it’s known to be the lair of a vicious sea 
creature that sometimes torments sailors. With the 
conch, though, Dunstuhl is sure the party will not only 
be safe, but they’ll be able to charm the beast, or 
whatever comes to the surface, into doing their 
bidding; however, Dunstuhl says he isn’t confident 
enough in himself to use the conch, and one of his new 
crew will have to do it. Have the party assign someone 
this task. 
 
Before that comes into play, though, the party will 
have to do something to ensure the creature comes up 
from the depths. This will be completely up to the 
creativity of the party. If they don’t come up with 
something themselves, have Dunstuhl suggest tying a 
rope around one of them and tossing them overboard 
as bait as if they’re fishing for the creature. [DM Note:  
This is a fairly predictable idea, so feel free to suggest 
whatever else comes to mind. That said, though, if the 
party requires Dunstuhl’s assistance rather than 
coming up with their own idea, give them a -4 when 
they roll on the Sea Encounter Table. When the party 
has decided on their tactic, you can have them roll 
whatever checks might be applicable for what they’re 
attempting to do: Strength for holding the rope for 
Dunstuhl’s idea, Nature for the person in the water to 
see if they’re convincing bait, Animal Handling if they 
decide to try to catch their own bait, and so on, 
deciding how to handle poor or exceptional roll 
outcomes as they happen; conversely, you can let the 
party roleplay this without rolling any dice and just 
allow their idea (or Dunstuhl’s) to work as intended.] 
 
When the party’s plan, whatever it may be, is put into 
action, have everyone roll a d20, add the values 
together, then divide the total by the number of party 
members to find the average. Use that average to 
determine exactly what will rise to engage your 
adventurers:

Now that the sea encounter is prepared, have the 
assigned conch blower roll a Performance check with a 
DC of the average the party rolled on the Sea 
Encounter Table minus four. [DM Note: For example, if 
they rolled an average of 14, they would have to 
succeed on a DC 10 Performance check to pacify those 
1d4 Plesiosauruses.] Give the party two chances to 
successfully complete the check, allowing them to roll 
with advantage if another party member offers to help. 
[DM Note: You may have Lord Dunstuhl suggest that 
someone do something to calm or inspire the 
adventurer blowing the conch if you so choose.] If they 
fail both checks, whatever they summoned will retreat, 
realizing something is wrong and these sailors aren’t 
safe to torment. If they succeed, Lord Dunstuhl will 
give their subdued minion(s) the following command: 
“Vicious <insert sea encounter here>, you now bend to 
the will of the great Kraken Priests! You will seek out 
the dread pirate Grandeur Kaine, and you will not be 
released until you taste his blood!” 
 
At this, Dunstuhl will empty a small vial into the water, 
causing whatever’s been charmed to go into a kind of 
frenzy and take off toward their prey, and the party 
sets sail after them. 
 
The Pirate Isles 
Regardless of how they get there, the party will 
eventually arrive near the marked location on the map. 
If they’ve successfully charmed a sea creature, they’ll 
find it wreaking havoc on Grandeur Kaine’s forces. If 
Lord Dunstuhl is still with the party, he will insist that 
they use the distraction to get to the treasure location; 
however, in spite of Dunstuhl’s insistence, or if he’s no 
longer present, the party may decide to join the fray 
and fight off the remaining pirates on Kaine’s ship, The 
Opulence. If this is the case, use the following table to 
determine how many pirates the adventurers will 
encounter (use the same number from the Sea 
Encounter Table): 
 
 Remaining Pirates Table 
1-2 4 Cannons, 6 Pirates + Grandeur Kaine 
3-4 2 Cannons, 6 Pirates + Grandeur Kaine 
5-6 6 Pirates + Grandeur Kaine

9-10 4 Pirates + Grandeur Kaine 
11-12 3 Pirates + Grandeur Kaine 
13-14 2 Pirates + Grandeur Kaine 
15-16 1 Pirate + Grandeur Kaine 
17-18 Grandeur Kaine 
19-20 None! 
 
[DM Note:  For this encounter, if the party faces canons 
(which isn’t likely, but possible) each member will 
suffer 1 damage per cannon every round that there’s a 
pirate not engaged and therefore free to fire the 
cannons.] Those that survive this fight will move on to 
the treasure location on the map to collect their riches. 
 
If the adventurers heed Dunstuhl’s advice and sail past 
their diversion, heading to the treasure location 
immediately, they will be able to sort through their 
loot unimpaired; however, as a crafty, dangerous 
pirate, Grandeur Kaine will ambush the party on the 
treasure island and try to take back what he believes to 
be rightfully his. 
 
Grandeur Kaine 
Grandeur Kaine is a stout, flamboyant halfling whose 
looks seem completely contrary to his reputation. He 
wears an oversized, wide-brimmed bush hat with 
feathers from some unknown exotic species of bird 
strapped to each side by a polished brass band. His 
ears are pierced several times, and his long, black hair 
is often overlooked for the extremely well-manicured, 
enormous handlebar mustache that extends past the 
edges of his face. His blue overcoat with brass 
adornments is dyed leather and covers a white shirt 
not unlike Lord Dunstuhl’s save it’s cinched at the top 
with a white handkerchief of the same delicate fabric. 
His pants and boots, while clearly high quality, are only 
a backdrop for the vibrant green dragon skin loin cloth 
he wears under his brass-buckled leather belt. 
Completing his look is the huge (for a halfling) rapier 
with intricately crafted basket guard that always seems 
to shimmer unnaturally.

If Dunstuhl is still with the party whenever they 
encounter Kaine, upon seeing each other Kaine will 
immediately say: 
“You…I should have known. This nonsense has your 
messy fingerprints all over it. I gave you a chance to 
start over! I gave you a chance to be free! You won’t get 
another…” 
 
Clearly these two have some history. [DM Note:  If the 
party encounters Kaine on his ship, he gets a +2 to all 
non-damage rolls.] 
 
Once the encounter with Kaine comes to its conclusion, 
if either Dunstuhl or Kaine are still standing, the 
adventures may pry as to the details of their previous 
relationship. Both will divulge that they were once 
partners, but Dunstuhl’s version of the story is that he 
was constantly disrespected and treated more like a 
pet than a companion so he escaped to plot his 
revenge; Kaine’s version (the truth) is that Dunstuhl’s 
mouth landed the crew in one too many unnecessary 
brawls and they were conspiring to take matters into 
their own hands. Essentially to save Dunstuhl’s life, 
Kaine fed his partner a strong sleeping potion so he 
appeared dead and tossed him overboard in a barrel 
with only his aforementioned items. [DM Note: It is 
very unlikely that Kaine will be left standing as pirates 
do not commonly become amicable with those that 
attempt to steal their treasure, but in D&D anything is 
possible!] 
 
When looted, Grandeur Kaine will yield 40 gp, his hat, 
his green dragonhide loin cloth, and his magical rapier, 
The Rapier of Grandeur (see stats below). 
 
The Treasure 
No matter how the adventure plays out, (hopefully?) the 
party should arrive at the X on the map. With Dunstuhl 
still along for the ride, the party won’t need to worry 
about the directions on the map; however, if he’s not 
around, the party will have to determine where exactly 
their treasure is hidden. As the directions vaguely state, 
the party is looking for an island with a single tree that 
also has a rock that molten copper has been poured 
over to make it appear like a wax seal. [DM Note:  If you 
have a group that likes to roll, you can have them roll for 
Perception or Investigation to find the island, and then 
they can roll for Strength, etc. to move the rock, dig up 
the treasure, unlock the chest, and so on; otherwise, 
just allow them to recognize the island once they’re in 
the right location on the map and simply talk them 
through unearthing and opening the treasure chest.] 
 
Treasure Contents 
The wooden chest opens with a cloud of dust and an acrid 
smell the assaults the nostrils. Upon looking inside, the 
adventurers find the following items: 
 Several bottles of vintage wine, one of which appears to 
have been broken when the chest was first buried. 
 Three wine-stained outfits in a similar style to what 
Grandeur Kaine was wearing. 
 A velvet pouch containing the bones of a large bird. 
 What appears to be a hand-sized cannon with a flint 
mechanism for igniting the flammable powder. 
 An incomplete spell scroll. 
 A note with a broken elven seal that simply reads, “IOU.” 
 500 gold dragons. 
The End 
Now that the treasure has been dispersed, this 
adventure has come to an end. If your party wants to 
move forward, you can pull on the string of the 
mysterious “IOU” to setup the next hook or follow the 
gold dragons back to Waterdeep where adventures are 
always waiting. Hopefully you’ve enjoyed running this 
adventure, and hopefully your players had a blast! Oh 
yeah, before I forget, if you’re asking yourself, “what 
about the Golden Mast?” well…it never existed, 
Dunstuhl made it up. The end. 
 
Final Notes 
The real beauty of playing Dungeons & Dragons lies in 
the simple truth that nearly anything can happen at any 
time. With that in mind, I tried not to go too overboard 
with options and explanations for what could happen as 
I want the game and story to feel as much yours as 
mine. I also didn’t include any specific game maps 
because I wanted to leave it up to the DM’s preference. 
Basically, though, all you’ll need is a medium/small ship 
for The Haberdasher, maybe a medium/large ship for 
The Opulence, and an island (preferably with one tree 
and a copper rock); however, depending on what the 
party does, other maps may become necessary. Luckily, 
the internet is an absolute treasure trove for all of 
these, and you’re likely to find something much better 
than I’d make myself anyway.
