INTRODUCTION 
The Avarsal is a short adventure that can be completed in 
one 4-hour session. It is designed for 1 st level characters. 
 The adventure is written for any mountainous region in 
the Forgotten Realms  setting, but is easily transferable to 
other worlds.  
For encounters, refer to the Monster’s Manual for 
monster stat blocks unless otherwise indicated.  
 
Aelwyn Avarsal , martyr of  Helm
 
HISTORY 
wo centuries ago a contingent of dwarves from 
the Rune clan was assigned to mine copper. 
Unhappy with their lot ( as much as dwarves  
love their fine copper kettles and pots, they 
would have rather been mining iron ore for 
arms and armor), they started a settlement at the base of 
the mountains near the mine and called it the Ruined 
Rune. An unexpected discovery of palladium may have 
changed their fortu nes, but it was not even a year before 
they were driven away by mountain orcs known as the 
Stonekin. 
A century later Aelwyn Avarsal, an elven high priest of 
Helm, founded the temple of Dawn’s Renewal at the site of 
the Ruined Rune. As with the dwarves, the Stonekin orcs 
spilled from the highest reaches of the mountains to 
destroy the guardians, smashing the temple to ruins. 
Aelwyn, the lone survivor, called upon Helm in her 
moment of need and  was heard. A searing light burst from 
her twin hammers, blinding much of the enemy force. 
Overcome with fur or, Aelwyn waded into the fray , 
defeating the orc leader and countless other enemies 
before finally being overwhelmed. When Aelwyn died Helm 
sent her spirit into the hammers and the light remained, 
permanently blinding and stunning  the Stonekin orcs and 
causing them to flee. The victory, witnessed by nomads 
and shared over the years, is  now chronicled as The Battle 
of Helm’s Light. 
Some fifty years later the ruins of Dawn’s Renewal were 
settled by gnome artificers  (glass specialists)  seeking the 
rumored palladium in the old dwarven mine  to toughen 
their glass products . The artificers discovered Aelwyn’s 
twin hammers buried in the temple ruins and built a 
contraption that magnified the holy light. Aware of the 
light’s benefit from local tales , the gnomes secured the 
contraption atop an old dwarven watchtower in the 
mountains, and with Helm’s light burning bright into the 
sky the Stonekin orcs stayed hidden in the mountains . The 
sentinel beacon atop the tower is now  known as the 
Avarsal.  
Despite their new innovation, the gnomes soon gathered 
the palladium they needed and moved on from the 
settlement. Under the protection of the Avarsal, new 
arrivals expanded the settlement into the town of 
Trademoot which became renowned for horse trading and 
wool production. As long as the light burned from the  
Avarsal, Trademoot w ould continue in peace and 
prosperity.  
SYNOPSIS 
The town of Trademoot is in danger. For fifty years , the 
Avarsal’s light has shone from the mountains , protecting 
the town from attack by the Stonekin orcs .  
But recently and without warning  the light has 
extinguished, leaving the inhabitants of Trademoot 
confused and fearful. And with good reason, for the 
Stonekin orcs have noticed the light’s absence . With its 
presence gone from the sky, the orcs  are no longer fearful 
of Avarsal’s Bane (detailed in the Magic Items section 
under Hammers of Helm) and have begun preparations for 
an assault. 
Unbeknownst to the inhabitants of Trademoot, the 
Avarsal has failed because a gnome artificer, Erwen Erwell, 
begrudged at being spurned by Blue-Eyed Sandy, traveled 
to the Avarsal with revenge in mind. There he ordered

CHAPTER 1 - 
TRADEMOOT 
READ-ALOUD TEXT  
Huddled under the shadow of ominous mountains, the 
village of Trademoot faces its perils from behind wind-
battered palisades and stone -hunched edifices.  
It is a tenuous and lonely existence  here between the edge 
of the tundra and the mountains, but it is lucrative for the 
frontier folk who call it ‘home.’  Noteworthy among them are 
the town’s famous horse -breeders and wool -textilers, whose 
herds and flocks dot the landscape . 
T1-SHEPHERD’S RUN 
A long stretch of uncultivated ryegrass and dried mud 
along the bank of Cold River, trampled and matted by the 
many flocks of sheep that spend twilight to morning 
curled up in rest here. The pair of buildings adjacent are a 
poor-weather barn and a  family residence.  
Shepherd’s Run has the distinct scent of fresh -cut grass 
and loamy soil-after-a-storm. During the day, blea ting 
can be heard across the river in the fields of orchard grass 
and white clover where the sheep are herded. The wool 
produced by the sheep of Trademoot is highly sought after.  
T2-OLD TOWN 
Nestled behind the palisade walls, Old Town is a cluster of 
small-inhabitant stone homes dating back to some of the 
early residents, artificer gnomes. Fashioned from striated -
limestone, these buildings have a brighter sheen than the 
more prevalent gray slate structures in the rest of town.  
T3-RUINED RUNE INN 
Cheap and bawdy, the Ruined Rune draws a hardened, 
fire-bellied crowd looking for hearth and drink after a long 
day’s labor. The inn is most fam ous for its excellent river 
trout dinner, homebrewed Avarsal Ale, and music.   
The Inn is the oldest establishment in Trademoot, 
dating back to the Ruined Rune dwarves , somehow 
surviving both orc invasions. Some think it is due to the  
Ruined Rune sigil in the basement. In fact, unbeknownst to 
anyone in Trademoot, the sigil is a Teleportation Circle 
(see Trademoot Features for more information)  
The inn is owned and operated  by Blue-Eyed Sandy, a 3rd 
level female human bard. Many residents are or were 
besotted with her , including the late Erwen Erwell. 
T4-MARKET SQUARE 
The market is a spacious cobblestone area where, once a 
ten-day, visiting tundra nomads, mountain barbarians 
and foresters set up their wares from tents and wagons.  
The imported goods i nclude wild game, dried fish, pipe 
weed, firewood, and furs.  
T5-CHANCERY 
A one-story, somewhat disheveled office, t he Chancery 
acts as the administration center and  council chamber for 
Trademoot. It is where all town deeds and manners of 
business are signed and settled , including the meeting 
with Lylwyn Shine and the council to discuss the Avarsal 
opportunity (see the Notable NPCs sidebar  on page six for 
more details).  
T6-WOOL MILLS 
The span of Wool Mills includes both watermills and 
windmills, the ma jority of which support the production of 
Trademoot’s most lucrative product by preparing, 
spinning, and weaving wool.  
Wool Mills also includes a gristmill and a stone oven.  
T7-HORSE TRADERS 
The raising grounds for the flourishing horse market, the 
Horse Traders expanse accommodates Muglundy Farms 
and Sasha’s, two competing interests that breed and sell 
horses and gear.  
Each outfit also has an operating blacksmith. Muglundy 
Farms has a wider variety of breeds, but Sasha’s are 
considered higher quality, a s reflected in the cost.  
WHAT TO DO IN TRADEMOOT 
The characters can explore the various areas of town both 
before and/or after meeting with Lylwyn Shine and the 
council to discuss the Avarsal business.  
Goods can be purchased in town for the going rates 
stipulated in Chapter 5 of the Player’s Handbook , with the 
exception of woolen fare and horses. Both are of 
exceptional quality, and cost 15 -25% more than normal . 
The Old Town Glassworks is a remnant of th e gnome 
artificers’ settlement prior to Trademoot, and may provide 
some areas of interest for the adventurers.

THE COUNCIL MEETING 
Not long after the adventurers arrive in town, they will be 
summoned to meet with the council . The council members 
present are Lylwyn Shine, Blue -Eyed Sandy, and 
Yellowknife (see Notable NPCs sidebar  for more details).  
Lylwyn Shine will be welcoming but also direct with the 
adventurers. Her first concern is the safety of Trademoot, 
and she will do whatever it takes to ensure that. She will 
offer the adventurers 100 gold pieces to investigate the 
Avarsal and return with news, and 400 additional gold 
pieces to relight it, 50 gp of that paid up-front. Due to the 
seriousness of the situation, she can be persuaded by the 
adventurers on a successfu l DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) 
check to sweeten the offer by 100 -200 gp, at the discretion 
of the DM. 
Yellowknife will off er her services as a guide through  
Deepwound Pass to the old copper mine. If the adventurers 
refuse her offer, she will instead warn them of wild 
weather, recent wolf sightings, and ‘ deadfalls’ (a thin layer 
of snow or weak slate that  hides deep crevices) in the 
mountain pass. She will also tell the adventurers that 
Stonekin Pass is currently impenetrable due to orc 
sightings and cannot b e taken. 
If the adventurers ask about the Avarsal, Lylwyn Shine 
will share Aelwyn’s legend as described in the second 
paragraph of the History section . She is not familiar with 
the artificers’ role or how the Avarsal works.

CHAPTER 2 – INTO THE 
MOUNTAINS 
READ-ALOUD TEXT (WINTER  SEASON ) 
The rock-strewn pass thrusts and jolts up the mountains like 
a deity’s thunderbolt, creating a capricious maze of ice -
walled dead ends, snow covered dead falls, and other yet 
unknown dangers.  
STONEKIN PASS 
The Stonekin Pass is a steep, winding route up the 
mountains that leads into the distant Stonekin orc 
territory. Orcs have been sighted in the pass of late in 
frightening numbers. 
Currently the pass is impenetrable due to its reclamation  
by the Stonekin orcs. The orcs are positioned with ranged 
weapons, cover, and high ground. This pass is not intended 
to be used by the adventurers , but provides a route for the 
threatening orc invasion .  
DEEPWOUND PASS 
The way to the Avarsal starts with  Deepwound Pass. The 
pass is considered Difficult Terrain and is too narrow at 
certain points for mounts.  
If Yellowknife is with the adventurers, t he travel time 
through the pass is two days, otherwise it is three . To 
determine encounters, r oll once from the t able during the 
first day and roll twice from the table during the second 
and third days of travel.
