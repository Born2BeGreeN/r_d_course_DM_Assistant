FR-DC-SCROG-03 Showdown At Starmetal Hills  (v1.0)        3 
 
ADVENTURE PRIMER 
“A good man is a hammer in the hand of Tyr.” 
Miltiades of Phlan 
Showdown At Starmetal Hills is designed for three to 
seven 1st-2nd-level characters and is optimized for 
five characters with an average party level (APL) of 1. 
Characters outside this level range cannot participate in 
this adventure. This adventure occurs in Longsaddle. 
BACKGROUND 
THE GOGAN BROTHERS GANG, a slick-tongued pack of 
no-good, thieving, backstabbing, sweet-smiling 
jackalweres, has robbed the GAMBLING GOLEM in 
LONGSADDLE. Ordinarily, that would be a fool’s errand.  
 Years ago, the Gogan boys tried something similar 
and got themselves caught and ran outta Longsaddle 
with their tails tucked. In response, the HARPELLS, a 
family of eccentric wizards and stalwart defenders of 
the frontier hamlet, wove powerful wards to alert them 
if any jackalwere ever came slinking back. 
 But them Gogans are wily. This time, they didn’t show 
up themselves. Instead, they sent some nasty help, a 
pack of kenku drifters known as THE LUSKAN CITY 
GANG. Mean as snakes and twice as ornery. The Gogan 
Brothers convinced the avian outlaws that Longsaddle 
was easy pickings. The kenku agreed to the alliance, but 
they ain’t planning on splitting the loot.  
 Them Luskan City birds strolled into the Gambling 
Golem in the morning and raised hell. By the time 
anyone could muster a response, the outlaws were 
gone, hightailing it toward the STARMETAL HILLS 
where the Gogan Brothers were waiting for them. Now 
the good folk of Longsaddle ain’t about to let that stand. 
They are fixing to put together a posse made up of 
ranchers, goat herders, and a few adventurers. There is 
coin to recover and justice to serve. 
OVERVIEW 
The adventure begins with a Call to Action scene and 
has one main Part. The adventure takes approximately 
1 hour to play. 
 
Call to Action. The posse arrives at a fork in the trail 
and decides to split up. Picking a path is the Call to 
Action.  
Part 1. The Pursuit. Depending on which path the 
characters choose to follow, they lead to different 
problems with the outlaws. Recovering the stolen 
coin lockbox or bringing the outlaws to justice is the 
Story Objective. 
ADVENTURE HOOK  
Use these hooks to explain why the characters are in 
Longsaddle. 
Longriders. The character has heard that Longsaddle is 
a place where fortunes are made. Out on these 
frontier lands, ranchers, farmers, and wizards are 
always looking to hire folks who can fend off 
monsters, cattle thieves, and the occasional band of 
marauding barbarians. 
Longsaddler. The character is or was a resident of 
Longsaddle or the surrounding lands. They know the 
local faces, the Harpells’ reputation, and just how 
weird things can get around these parts. They might 
be just as strange themselves and have been accepted 
as part of the community all the same. 
Faction Agent. The character is working with one of the 
many factions of Faerûn, an adventuring society, or a 
guild. Either way, they are temporarily stationed in 
Longsaddle between missions, gathering information, 
maintaining a presence, or awaiting new orders. 
Harpell Apprentice. The character is currently staying 
at Ivy Mansion as part of an apprenticeship. Under 
the tutelage of the Harpells, they’ve been learning 
spells, dodging experiments gone wrong, and helping 
contain magical accidents. When the Harpells need 
someone to handle a strange task or investigate a 
disturbance, it’s usually the apprentices who get 
volunteered. 
GOGAN BROTHERS GANG FEUD 
At certain points in the adventure, the Story Award (Gogan 
Brothers Gang Feud) (from FR-DC-SCROG-02 Bad Weather 
Over Longsaddle) may influence the unfolding events. If none 
of the characters possess this story award, simply ignore any 
references to it.

PART 1: THE PURSUIT  
Estimated Duration. 45 minutes 
 
The characters must decide which trail to follow, either 
the hoofprints leading west or the boot prints heading 
east. This choice sets the direction of the adventure and 
determines what encounters lie ahead. Once they 
choose a trail, they must also decide whether to press 
their mounts to catch up quickly or travel at a safer pace 
through the rugged terrain. Pushing their horses may 
help them close the distance, but it carries the risk of 
injuring their mounts before they reach the thieves.  
STORY OBJECTIVE 
To recover the stolen coin lockbox or bring the outlaws 
to justice is the Story Objective. 
THAT’S SOME FANCY RIDING 
Read aloud or paraphrase the following: 
The Starmetal Hills roll out ahead like the spine of some 
long-dead beast, all broken ridgelines and sunbaked stone. 
Wind hisses through dry grass and thorny brush, stirring 
dust devils that dance across the trail. The sun glares down 
from a cloudless sky, already high and hot, with maybe 
eight hours of daylight left before the hills go dark. 
AREA INFORMATION 
The hills have the same general features outlined in the 
Call to Action. Add the following features. 
Visibility. During daylight, characters can see up to 2 
miles unless terrain features such as rocks, ridgelines, 
or brush block their view. If a character climbs to the 
top of a high hill, they can see up to 40 miles in any 
direction. Flying creatures may be able to spot their 
quarry from above. The horses heading west are 
approximately 3 miles away, while the group on foot 
is about 2 miles out. Due to the distance, only vague 
figures or movement can be seen. The number of 
individuals or specific details remain unclear, and a 
flying creature cannot determine whether the horses 
are mounted or simply running loose unless they get 
closer. 
Tracks. Following the tracks is fairly easy during the 
day. However, at night, limited visibility and uneven 
terrain make tracking more difficult. At night, a 
tracker can use the Search action to make a Wisdom 
(Survival) check against DC 15, with advantage due to 
the abundance of tracks. On a success, the tracker 
follows the trail. On a failure, the trail is lost, but the 
character can attempt to locate it again by carefully 
searching the area for one hour. 
 
TIME LIMIT 
The characters are in pursuit of outlaws, and time is not on 
their side. The thieves have split into two groups in an effort 
to throw off pursuit. One group travel on horseback, moving 
fast across open land. The other slips away on foot, sticking to 
gullies, creek beds, and cover. Fortunately for the pursuers, 
the mounted outlaws ran into trouble and lost an hour of 
travel, while the ones on foot spent more time arguing than 
moving. The posse cannot follow both. The characters must 
decide which group to chase. The coin lockbox could be with 
either group. And if the characters spend too long deciding, 
they may lose both trails entirely. 
TRACKING TIME 
Here is a timeline of relevant events to help track the pursuit 
and determine when key encounters occur: 
8:00 AM – The outlaws rob the Gambling Golem and flee 
Longsaddle, heading toward the Starmetal Hills. 
10:00 AM – The posse departs Longsaddle in pursuit. 
1:00 PM – The posse arrives at the point where the tracks 
split. One set of tracks lead into rough terrain on foot, while 
the other set of tracks is made by horses. 
9:00 PM – Sundown. Visibility drops, and tracking becomes 
significantly harder without magical aid or proper light 
sources. Around this time, the outlaws begin to make camp. 
3:00 AM (Optional) – The Gogan Brothers murder the outlaws 
and flee into the Starmetal Hills.  
5:00 AM (Optional) – The Luskan City Gang mount their 
horses and continue south toward Triboar.  
7:00 AM – The posse regroups to compare findings and plan 
the next steps in the pursuit. 
 As a Search action, a character can inspect the tracks 
and make a DC 10 Wisdom (Survival) check. On a 
success, the character can confirm the same details 
Athena has already shared about the split in the trail 
and the outlaws’ movements. If a character beats the DC 
by 5 or more, they notice something Athena missed: 
unusual tracks in the nearby brush. Three sets of prints 
begin as animal tracks before shifting into humanoid 
boot prints. Whoever they are, they’re following the 
outlaws on foot.  
 A character may take the Study action and make a DC 
13 Intelligence (Nature) check on the animal tracks. On 
a success, they identify the prints as belonging to a 
jackal. A character may make a DC 11 Intelligence 
(Arcana) check to recall that several creatures are 
capable of such transformations. Shapeshifters such as 
jackalweres and lycanthropes, certain fiends, druids, or 
even spellcasters using polymorph magic could all be 
responsible.  
 The NPC chosen by the characters to accompany 
them confirms that the tracks belong to jackalweres. 
They explain that these jackalweres are likely the Gogan 
Brothers Gang, a slippery crew of outlaws known up 
and down the Long Road. The gang isn’t made up of 
blood kin, but they all call each other “brother” and run 
their outfit like a big, rowdy family. They’re also made 
up entirely of jackalweres. 
 The characters are mounted on either a riding horse 
or a puddlejumper (giant frog).

 In difficult terrain, a riding horse travels at a fast pace 
of 4 miles per hour, a normal pace of 3 miles per hour, 
or a slow pace of 2 miles per hour. Puddlejumpers 
aren’t affected by difficult terrain of the Starmetal Hills 
and have the same travel pace as a riding horse. The 
mounted outlaws move at a normal pace of 3 miles per 
hour but are only one hour ahead due to mishaps while 
trying to manage so many horses. The outlaws on foot 
move at a normal pace of 1.5 miles per hour but are two 
hours ahead. Both groups of outlaws stop to camp 
around 9:00 PM as night falls. 
• At a normal pace, the mounted characters can catch 
up to the outlaws on foot within two hours. Reduce 
the time by one hour if they move at a fast pace or 
add four hours if they move at a slow pace. 
• At a normal pace, the mounted characters arrive at 
the camp of the mounted outlaws within ten hours. 
Reduce the time by one hour if they move at a fast 
pace or add two hours if they move at a slow pace. 
• If the characters are on foot (due to losing their 
mounts because of riding complications or choosing 
to go on foot), you can estimate that the characters 
arrive at the outlaw camps around 11:00 PM for the 
outlaws on foot, or around 1:00 AM for the outlaws 
on horseback. These estimates assume the characters 
are traveling at a normal pace. Reduce the time by 
one hour if they move at a fast pace or add two hours 
if they move at a slow pace. 
Note that traveling at a fast pace imposes disadvantage 
on a traveler’s Wisdom (Perception or Survival) and 
Dexterity (Stealth) checks. Traveling at a normal pace 
imposes disadvantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks.  
Traveling at a slow pace grants advantage on Wisdom 
(Perception or Survival) checks. 
 If the characters decide to press their mounts to 
move faster (doubling their speed), they can reach the 
quarry within 1d10 + 30 minutes. Once a mount has 
been pushed to double its speed, it cannot be pushed 
again until it has completed a Short or Long Rest.  
 To navigate the terrain safely at high speed, each 
rider must attempt a DC 13 skill check. They may 
choose one of the following skills for the roll: Strength 
(Athletics), Intelligence (Investigation), Wisdom 
(Animal Handling), or Wisdom (Perception). The NPC 
posse member is assumed to succeed automatically. 
Any character who fails their check must roll on the 
Riding Complications table to determine what goes 
wrong during the ride. 
TABLE 1.1 RIDING COMPLICATIONS 
1d6 Complication 
1 A mount slips on a patch of crumbling rock and 
takes 7 (2d6) bludgeoning damage from the fall. 
The rider must succeed on a DC 12 Dexterity 
saving throw or be thrown from the saddle, 
taking 3 (1d6) bludgeoning damage and falling 
prone. 
2 A snake sunning on a rock sends the mount into 
a panic. The rider must succeed on a DC 13 
Animal Handling check to calm the beast. On a 
failure, the rider is thrown off the mount, taking 
3 (1d6) bludgeoning damage and falling prone. 
The mount runs off in a panic. Characters can 
track it down with a DC 10 Wisdom (Survival) 
check, but the search takes 1d4 x 10 minutes.  
3 A mount trips on a rock, does a full front-flip, 
and somehow lands on its feet. The rider must 
make a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw or be flung 
into a cactus, taking 5 (2d4) piercing damage. 
4 The rider charges through a hidden burrow field, 
upsetting a swarm of furious groundhogs. The 
character must succeed on a DC 13 Animal 
Handling check or be swarmed. The mount takes 
5 (2d4) piercing damage from a dozen tiny bites, 
and the rider’s pants are either shredded beyond 
repair or torn clean off. The swarm stops 
attacking after a round and flees back into their 
burrows.  
5 The rider’s mount gets nervous and starts letting 
out a series of loud, noxious farts. The rider and 
the mount directly behind (chosen at random) 
must each make a DC 12 Constitution saving 
throw or suffer the poisoned condition for the 
next hour from the overwhelming stench. It’s 
that bad.  
6 The mount plunges into a collapsed sinkhole, ten 
feet wide and twenty feet deep. It takes 7 (2d6) 
bludgeoning damage from the fall. The rider 
must succeed on a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw 
to leap free. On a failure, they fall in as well, 
taking 7 (2d6) bludgeoning damage and landing 
in a heap beside their mount. 
THE LOCKBOX 
It does not really matter which group the characters decide to 
chase. Coincidentally, or perhaps by the will of Tymora, the 
group they pursue just so happens to be the one carrying the 
stolen coin lockbox from the Gambling Golem.  
 The lockbox is protected by a Lock of Trickery imbued with 
the Unbreakable minor property. The only way to destroy the 
lock is with an adamantine weapon. The lockbox itself is not 
indestructible. It has 15 hit points and weighs 40 lbs. due to 
the coins inside. Inside there are 2,000 copper pieces, a tin 
harmonica (trinket), a bottle of snake oil (trinket), and a 
handful of crumpled IOUs, each scribbled with a name: the 
Slayer, Corvus Ravenwinter, Bertie, Kipper, Regis, Badmoon, 
and Larry.  
 Now, if the characters are feeling a bit crooked, they might 
hang onto that coin lockbox (and the contents inside). Their 
Bidderdoo companion doesn’t care about the coin lockbox or 
what the characters intend to do with it. If the characters do 
keep the enchanted lock, they eventually find the key in 
Longsaddle after the adventure.   
DEVELOPMENT 
If the characters catch up to either group of outlaws 
before they make camp, proceed to Encounter A. 
If the characters arrive at the camp of the outlaws, 
proceed to Encounter B.

ENCOUNTER A. OUTLAW PURSUIT 
If the characters catch up with either group of outlaws 
on the move, then run this encounter. Read or 
paraphrase the following:  
The trail winds between two steep hills. Jagged rocks and 
clusters of scrub line the uneven slope. Large boulders jut 
from the hillside, creating short pockets of shadow along 
the path.  
AREA INFORMATION 
This location has the following features. 
Terrain. The trail leads through the hills, where the 
slope rises sharply on all sides. 
Slopes. All slopes count as difficult terrain. 
Obstacles. The surrounding terrain is dotted with 
natural cover. Large boulders and scattered stone 
outcroppings offer protection to anyone approaching 
the ridge. A creature behind one of these features 
may gain cover, depending on their position. 
Lighting. During daylight hours, the area is bathed in 
bright light from the sun.  
Map. Use map 3.1 (Appendix 1) for this encounter.  
DEVELOPMENT 
If the characters followed the mounted group, proceed 
to The Mounted Outlaws. If they followed the group on 
foot, proceed to The Outlaws on Foot. 
THE MOUNTED OUTLAWS 
The adventurers come upon five riding horses grazing 
nervously on the hillside. One horse is missing, lost 
earlier when an outlaw had a mishap with a 
cantankerous earth elemental that did not take kindly 
to being stepped on. The elemental killed both the horse 
and its rider.  
 A raven circling overhead is actually a familiar. It 
serves as an aerial reconnaissance for its kenku master, 
keeping watch from about 200 feet in the air. If the 
raven is attacked and survives, it immediately takes the 
Dodge action and attempts to fly away. If the raven is 
killed, it cannot warn its master. As a result, the 
mounted outlaws are caught off guard and are easily 
surprised when the characters arrive. 
 The Luskan City outlaws (kenku) are hiding nearby, 
warned by the raven familiar that the characters are 
close. They have taken cover behind rocky outcroppings 
and plan to ambush the party as soon as they come 
within range of their ranged attacks. Any character with 
a Passive Perception of 14 or higher notices movement 
behind the rocks and is not surprised. 
 If the characters killed the raven familiar, the outlaws 
have dismounted to water their horses and unaware of 
the characters approach. The characters can choose to 
ambush the outlaws, who have a Passive Perception of 
15. On initiative count 11, the outlaws’ horses panic and 
flee into the hills.  
ADJUSTING THE SCENE  
Here are some suggestions for adjusting this scene. The 
adjustments are cumulative: 
 
• Very Weak. Remove one kenku.  
• Weak. Remove one kenku. 
• Average. Five kenku.  
• Strong. Add one kenku wizard (pirate deck wizard).   
• Very Strong. Add another kenku wizard (pirate deck 
wizard).  
THE BIDDERDOO 
The Bidderdoo won’t reveal their nature unless the situation 
becomes dire (such as the characters losing or being 
outnumbered). In combat, while in human form, the 
Bidderdoo only provides the Help action to assist the 
characters. 
ADJUSTING THE ADVENTURE 
If they are following the horse tracks it is possible for the 
characters to press their mounts to catch the mounted 
outlaws, then use the outlaws’ horses to pursue the second 
group. However, the only way this plan succeeds is if the 
characters press the outlaw horses to double their speed for 
one hour, allowing them to eventually catch up with the 
outlaws on foot.   
 This method allows the characters to chase down both 
groups within the same session, although it may stretch the 
adventure session beyond one hour. 
 The other members of the posse won’t be of any help. 
Athena is preoccupied with tracking down missing posse 
members who wandered off into the Starmetal Hills and got 
themselves lost. 
 If the characters choose to pursue and defeat both 
groups, only the first group they confront carries any 
treasure. The coin lockbox is also in the possession of 
that first group. 
THE OUTLAWS ON FOOT 
If the characters were tracking the outlaws on foot, they 
find the Luskan City outlaws (kenku) and the 
jackalweres, the latter in their human forms. The two 
groups are locked in a heated argument over the coin 
lockbox. The Gogan Brothers want to force it open but 
worry that breaking the lockbox might damage 
whatever valuables lie inside. The Luskan City outlaws, 
unwilling to hand it over, insist on keeping the lockbox 
until they can discover a magical way to unlock it. They 
have a passive Perception of 15, but characters 
attempting to approach may do so with advantage on 
their Stealth checks.

ADJUSTING THE SCENE  
Here are some suggestions for adjusting this scene. The 
adjustments are cumulative: 
 
• Very Weak. Remove one kenku.  
• Weak. Remove one kenku and one jackalwere.  
• Average. Three kenku, two jackalwere.  
• Strong. Add one jackalwere. 
• Very Strong. Add one giant jackal (giant hyena). 
THE BIDDERDOO 
The Bidderdoo won’t reveal their nature unless the situation 
becomes dire (such as the characters losing or being 
outnumbered). In combat, while in human form, the 
Bidderdoo only provides the Help action to assist the 
characters. 
GOGAN BROTHERS GANG FEUD 
Any character with the Story Award (Gogan Brothers Gang 
Feud) is immediately recognized by the jackalweres as an 
enemy of their gang. The shapeshifters prioritize targeting 
these characters first.  
OUTLAW NAMES SUGGESTIONS 
Jackalwere: Bonnie Red Smoke, Delgrave the Hook, Big Iron 
Crowe, Jedidiah, Cassidy Six-Fingers, Maybelle Witch-Eye, 
Pearl the Grin, or Ezekiel Harrow 
Kenku: Cough, Snap, Clang, Hell, Old Timer, Click Click Click, 
Witch Eye, Hush, Tink Tink, Scratch, Tom, or Rusty 
TREASURE 
The treasure for both encounters is the same. 
Characters that search the bodies of the outlaws find the 
following: a ten-gallon hat (trinket), a very confused 
toad (see sidebar), a pair of fancy snakeskin boots 
(trinket), and a colorful thin art book titled Billy the Kid 
versus Strahd, filled with pulpy illustrations (trinket). 
Rounding out the haul is twelve different rabbit foot 
charms; each dyed a different color (trinkets).  
BATTLETOAD BILL 
It is an unsavory pastime in Longsaddle, but toads are often 
used in animal fighting sports. The toad the characters 
recovered from the outlaws is no ordinary critter. It is a 
retired champion brawler known as Battletoad Bill, now freed 
from that cruel business. If the characters choose to keep the 
toad and take the time to care for it properly, they gain a loyal 
pet and an unlikely companion. The characters receive the 
Story Award: Battletoad. 
DEVELOPMENT 
Once the characters have defeated the band of outlaws 
and retrieved the coin lockbox, they may proceed to 
Conclusion. If any jackalweres are still alive, go to The 
Protector in Encounter B.
