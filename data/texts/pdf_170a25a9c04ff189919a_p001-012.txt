A VERY GOBLIN GIFTSMAS
A Festive introductory Adventure By seAn Goodison
In this one-shot adventure, the player’s are a band of 
goblin misfits taking part in a midnight raid on a sleepy 
elven village.
A 3-4 hour adventure for 4-7 new players


[PDF_PAGE=2]
Introduction
This adventure is designed to be played as a one-off 
session. It could be used as a break from your regular 
campaign, giving your experienced players a chance 
to  play around with some simpler, more expendable 
characters; or as an introduction to the basic gameplay 
of Dungeons & Dragons, perhaps for curious family 
members and friends.
The goal of the adventure (steal elven gifts) as well as 
the obstacle (sleeping residents and watchful guards) 
are set up very quickly before players are dropped into 
an open-ended phase of the game. As the DM, it will be 
up to you to roll with whatever schemes your goblin-
minded players concoct. Don’t be afraid to go big with 
any consequences that might arise; this is a one-shot 
after all, so no-one’s going to mind if half the village 
burns down.
The main measure of success for your players will 
be knowing just how much treasure they were able 
to steal. To this end, try awarding them something 
physical (chocolate coins are perfect) that they can 
hold onto. This has the added benefit of avoiding any 
downtime during play while players add treasure to 
their inventories.
When your session’s alloted time draws near, the final 
phase of the session will disrupt whatever schemes are 
still running as an obvious ‘boss-battle’ opportunity 
presents itself. This is a great chance for the fractious 
goblins to come together and cooperate against a final 
challenge.
Once the final battle is over, the players will have 
a chance to recount some of their great deeds in 
character. Make sure everyone gets a say before 
announcing that the game has ended and thanking 
them for playing. Having this moment to reflect on 
highlights from the session will encourage players to 
continue talking about and sharing the experience 
even after it has formally ended.
Thank you for trying out A Very Goblin Giftsmas. I 
would love to hear how your sessions play out, and 
what schemes your PCs come up with. You can find me 
on Twitter @HighScoreKid
One Page Adventure
One of the goals of this adventure’s design was to fit 
all the DM’s information onto a single page in order 
to cut down on page-flipping and to keep focus on the 
action at the table. Naturally, this means that much of 
the adventure isn’t as fleshed out as you might be used 
to seeing. DMs, being the infinitely creative souls that 
they are, will no doubt be able to fill in the blanks.

combat statistics condensed down to save space. The 
elements of this stat block are as follows:

(7) Attack Name, (8) Bonus to hit, (9)Amount of damage,

A Very Goblin Giftsmas
• Players choose a character sheet each.
• Randomly assign goblin names and keep them hidden 
for now.
It is the shortest day of the year and the forest is blanketed 
in thick white snow. As the sun sets, the Oozing Wound 
tribe gathers before its chief.
“My friends, it has come to my attention that the village 
of the Duskvale Elves lies ripe for raiding. Our scouts have 
informed me that the fey-folk have spent the entire day 
feasting and drinking and have fallen into a deep slumber. 
What’s more, the scouts observed the elves exchanging a 
hoard of tiny chests, crates and boxes. No doubt full of 
elvish trinkets!”
The chief gestures towards the growing crowd, “Who 
among you is brave enough to join the raid?
“Tell us of a great deed you have performed and you shall 
earn your place.”
• Players take turns introducing their character and 
creating a quick story of a great deed they have 
performed in the past.
• The Chief then sends them off to the raid.
PLAY IT OUT
Ignore initiative, cycle play around the table.
The village consists of three guard towers, 9 small 
houses and one large house. There are two Elf Guards 
in the large house and one in each tower. Each 
house hosts 2 Elf Villagers and 2 Elf Children (non-
combatants) and has a pile of 1d8 gifts in the main 
room. The large house has a feasting hall still laid out 
with food, any goblin can scoff some food as an action 
and gain a one-time bonus of 10 temporary hit points.
Any elf who spots a lone goblin will move to engage, 
shouting to wake anyone nearby. An elf who sees more 
than one goblin will try to run to the large house and 
ring an alarm bell.
Once the alarm bell is rung, Elf Villagers will become 
Elf Militia and appear at a rate of 1d4-2 per round, 
moving towards the alarm.
A huge pine tree stands in the centre of the village 
and has 3d6 gifts beneath it.
On the outskirts of the village is a Dryad named 
Mistletoe who is deathly bored and wants to see some 
romance in exchange for a gift. If two goblins agree and 
kiss in front of her, Mistletoe says, “The greatest gift 
of all is love, may it warm your hearts for evermore.” 
She then disappears with no further word. While the 
gift seems like a trick, the two participating goblins will 
automatically save against Klaus’ slowing wind when 
he shows up later, as love keeps them warm.
• With one hour left, the Spirit of Winter, Klaus, arrives 
wherever the goblins are concentrated, alongside his 
two flying elk. He seeks to kidnap a number of them 
for his workshop but will flee if reduced below 20 hp.
• Any remaining goblins return to the village where 
the chief asks each player to recount something 
incredible they did during the raid.
