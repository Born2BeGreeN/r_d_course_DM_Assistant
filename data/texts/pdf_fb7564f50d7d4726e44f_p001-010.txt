Background & Setting 
This adventure series is set in and around the 
lands adjoining the Nether Mountain Range of 
The Silver Marches. Halved by the River 
Rauvin, these lands are a racial melting pot of 
the elves, human, halflings, dwarves, gnomes, 
orc’s and the occasional kobold or goblin.  
Relations between the more “civilised” races 
and those of a more “barbaric” nature, often 
are strained to say the least. Orcish raids 
impact upon elvish and human settlements. 
Dwarvish interests, laying below ground, are 
less targeted by creatures living above, yet 
face their own set of perilous foes. 
The River Rauvin acts as a trade route cutting 
through the area, with numerous settlements 
dotting its fertile banks. To the north, the 
Nether Mountains act as an imposing 
boundary, full of varying races and the odd 
blue dragon. South of the river, lies the 
expanse of the dense and somewhat sacred 
High Forrest. Rumoured to house at least two 
Uhtgarde Barbarian tribes, ancient elvish cities 
and numerous ruins.  
Civilised points of interest within this small 
region include the “Gem of the North”, 
Silverymoon, Everlund the merchant city, 
Sundabar the subterranean dwarvish city, and 
numerous smaller villages and towns.  
Our story is to begin in the small resolute 
village of Jalanthar. This small village of some 
300 souls features a number of humans, elves 
halflings and 3 or 4 half-orcs.  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
This village’s primary occupations include 
hunting and trapping for fur through the 
nearby foothills. Local’s also often act as 
guides for various Waterdhavian Noble 
hunting parties, or various merchant folk 
travelling throughout the land.   
The village is troubled from time to time by 
the various orcish tribes that inhabit the 
mountain’s to the north. Being used to such 
antics however, the village doesn’t often allow 
themselves to be bested by such a raid.  
Players wishing to create characters to suit 
such a setting have a plethora of options 
available to them. Both racially and in terms of 
background, players could draw on various 
sources of inspiration from the industrious 
dwarven miners of nearby Sundabar to the 
mythical Uhtgardian Barbarian tribes of the 
Highforest. Additionally, due to the high levels 
of mercantile activity within the region, 
strange visitors or travellers often happen by 
even the smallest, villages.  
Ideally however, each of these characters 
should have a reason for finding themselves in 
the small village of Jalanthar, and ultimately, a 
desire to leave sooner, rather than later. 
Desires for wanting to leave this area or town 
at least could include: 
 A lack of suitable work  
 Wonderlust  
 Running from a debt or problem 
 Person quest

Introduction 
 
The Merchant’s Quest 
 Grilop is nowhere to be seen the following 
morning as the sun rises over the lower peaks 
of the Nether Mountain range. His previous 
ranting of not being able to wait to leave this 
town, seem to have softened as mid-morning 
eventually rolls around. In the soft autumn sun 
eventually a gruff stomping Grilop emerges 
from the inn holding his head. A stable boy 
runs from behind the inn with a medium sized 
cart towed by two large draft horses.  
Grilop turns around after barking at the boy to 
not waste his time, then to face the assembled 
party. Scrutinising each of characters with 
bloodshot eyes, he begins to lay out a number 
of “rules” for the upcoming travel. They 
mostly entail how not to make him angry, care 
of the horses, maintaining a watch at all times, 
protecting the wagon and other trivial tasks. 
Grilop grunts that the pay is 15gp each for 
accompanying the cart and providing a full 
and safe delivery to the next town of 
Lhuvenhead, which lay around 4 days travel to 
the west. He snorts at the party, stating that he 
has seen more professional goblin bands than 
the riff-raff standing before him.  
With that he turns, mounts the driver’s seat on 
the wagon and gives the reins a flick, urging 
the draft horses onwards. The party must hurry 
to keep up. 
You, for whatever reason, find yourself 
milling around Jalanthar restlessly. With 
almost no work around for a strong back, or 
dexterous hand, the village has little to offer 
the few idle people such as yourself. In which 
case you seem to find yourself gathering in or 
around the well-known Crowing Cockatrice 
inn at the end of each day to catch any news 
of work.  
Through pure chance or perhaps fate, as some 
may deem it, a merchant who frequents the 
village, on a monthly routine, has recently 
had a disagreement with his regular “muscle” 
and is looking for a number of capable 
persons to accompany him through the next 
leg of his route.  
This merchant has a somewhat dubious 
reputation as being greedy, arrogant and 
purely self-serving. Named Grilop Dungree 
he is a balding, silver haired, middle aged 
human, disenfranchised by his own reality. 
Rumour has it that over the years and constant 
irritations from raiding orc’s, pillaging 
bandits or pure bad luck, Grilop makes close 
to enough money to happily retire before 
having his cart burnt, his wares ransacked or 
his purse lightened by feverous drinking and 
gambling binges in Silverymoon. These 
events lead Grilop back to the roads and his 
merchant ways, destined it seems, to grind 
this path continually till his own demise. 
Grilop has announced throughout the inn this 
past evening, during a series of drunken 
ranting full of vulgarity, that he will be 
leaving the village at the crack of dawn, for 
his next destination. Should anyone be 
interested in accompanying him, he can offer 
15gp for each person. He continues with, 
“Although the pay may seem low in terms of 
gold pieces, the opportunity to leave a fetid 
backwater town such as this one, is in itself a 
priceless opportunity. Should anyone be 
interested in accompanying me for protection 
for coin, should be there dressed and prepared 
for anything in front of the inn when I leave.” 
Roleplaying Grilop Dungree: 
Grilop is a bitter old man, dissatisfied with 
his lot in life, even though he is partially to 
blame for it. He is gruff, dishonest, and 
devious when it comes to dealing with 
others and will often try to squeeze extra 
coin in any opportunity. Grilop should react 
poorly to being questioned or disagreed 
with. Additionally he will typically criticise 
anything a character does or tries. He is 
lazy and will expect the party to prepare 
camp or anything else menial.  
Grilop should be a tyrannical employer and 
should pick on one specific character over 
all the others. This can lead to some 
interesting party politics or dynamics.

Journey to Lhuvenhead 
With around an hour of daylight still 
remaining the party is able to sort out anything 
camp related before settling in for the night. 
As the suns last rays can be seen on the tip of 
the Nether Mountains to the North have the 
party make a Wisdom (Perception) Check. 
The character with the highest roll, will notice 
in the distance to the north, moving through 
the field towards the wagon, three orc’s (MM 
pg.246) in light armour carrying javelins. The 
Orcs are unaware they have been seen and are 
currently 120ft to the north. 
These orc’s each bear an effigy on their 
tattered leather armour which can be identified 
with a DC12 Intelligence (History) Check. 
The symbol represents the tribe of the 
“Thousand Fists”. A particularly blood thirsty 
tribe notorious within this region. A successful 
roll of DC15 or over will additionally identify 
these orcs as being scouts, as determined by 
their armour and weapons.      
Defeating these orc scouts will yield 300xp 
shared between the party members. One orc 
can be found wearing an amethyst crystal the 
size of a man’s thumb. This crystal can be 
used as an Arcane Focus and is worth 35gp. 
The orcs are primarily interested in the furs 
visibly loaded on the wagon. They will attempt 
to draw the party away from the wagon with 
hit and run tactics, while one of the orcs 
attempts to steal as many furs as possible. 
Should Grilop discover the next morning that 
any of the furs are missing, he will deduct 5gp 
from each of the characters pay.  
The rest of the first night proceeds without 
incident, however the character on watch may 
be nervous about nearby wolf howls heard 
throughout the night. Grilop arises with dawn 
this morning as the poisonous grasp of alcohol 
has run its course. He kicks any member of the 
party still sleeping till they rise and swiftly 
goes about telling the party to hurry up with 
the camp as they have many miles to make up 
today.  
At this point the DM should choose a 
responsible character to receive a message 
found wrapped around an ornate dagger 
beside their head as they awake. The message 
reads as follows:  
Travelling to the next town will take around 
three and a half days due to the slow start the 
previous day. During this time for each 
morning and afternoon DM’s should roll a 1d4 
to determine if a random encounter should 
take place. Odds mean there is no encounter 
and Evens rolled determine there is. 
Encounters in this region can be derived from 
the following table: 
Roll 
1d8 Consequences 
1-2 4 wolves hunt the wagon 
3-4 1 ogre strolling through the world 
5-6  2 orcs roaming around for food 
7-8 4 kobolds tunnelling near the road 
 (Creature Locations: Wolf MM pg.341, Ogre MM pg.237, 
Orc pg.246 & Kobolds pg.195)  
The remainder of the day spent travelling 
from Jalanthar is quiet and uneventful. The 
countryside consists of rolling hills with 
typical farm land to either side of the narrow 
and rutted roadway. Grilop remains in the 
driver’s seat for the entire journey nursing a 
seemingly wicked hangover, judging by the 
constant groans and rubbing of his temples. 
The party finds themselves able to converse 
relatively freely, as long doing so quietly 
around Grilop, which gives them a chance to 
introduce themselves and their stories.  
As the sun begins to fall towards the horizon 
Grilop states that camp should be made just 
off the road here. He pulls in the horses to a 
small clearing, reaches into the cart for a 
bedroll, and painfully descends from his 
position on the wagon before unrolling his 
makeshift bed and crawling in to sleep 
immediately.  
 
Grilop has few friends due to his stingy 
nature. Take care following such a greedy 
selfish fool… Don’t lose this dagger, I’ll 
be back for it shortly.  
Snuf.

The wolves will attempt to attack any 
character slightly separated from the group. 
The ogre will attempt to charge and attack the 
horses for food. 
The orcs will attempt to steal from the cart. 
The kobolds will attempt to lead players away 
with poorly aimed arrows while 2 raid the cart.  
Each of these encounters should grant 25xp 
per character and 1 reward from the 
Individual Treasure: Challenge 0-4 Table 
from the DMG pg.136. 
Characters should be very near to level 2 
before they reach the village of Lhuvenhead. If 
desired, the DM may wish to add additional 
challenges to the party, should they be coping 
easily with the current challenges.  
Lhuvenhead 
 Within the town of Lhuvenhead are 3 small 
stores, the Inn and a very modest number of 
homes scattered through the overgrown ruins. 
Any local will be able to explain that the ruins 
are due to a massive orc raid which occurred 
around 8 or 9 years ago now which essentially 
ruined the town. Few still remain from what 
was a vibrant river trading town. Folk here 
seem to be weary of Grilop and his market 
stand. Seemly having had poor experiences 
with him before. NPC characters about town 
may inform the party on multiple occasions to 
avoid trusting him too much. 
Lhuvenhead Shops and Characters  
Iron Piercer (smithy): Blacksmith which 
specialises in arrowheads and knives/daggers 
and also regular horse tack. Run by a young 
dark bearded dwarf named Punket Ironheart. 
This store sells arrows, daggers and Horse 
Tack at 15% off the regular price. Punket, will 
also offer the party a quest to identify some 
strange script he found chiselled on a large 
slab of stone outside of town. He shows the 
party a small piece of parchment which he had 
copied it onto. The script is in dwarvish, but 
the letters it depicts form words which are not 
recognisable. Characters able to shed any light 
on the script receive a free dagger and 35xp.  
The script is in Giant, characters who 
understand this language are able to read it 
immediately. Characters can make a DC15 
Intelligence (History) check, to uncover the 
script as giant. But they are unable to decipher 
it. The script reads: “To the Sky for the Seat of 
Ostoria”   
With a DC18 Intelligence (History) check, a 
character can recall that Ostoria was the 
Empire of the Giants, in the times before 
dwarves, men and elves.  
Herbal Help (alchemist): An overgrown 
shack seemingly standing from before the orc 
attack. Run by an old elf named Quina 
Aedwin. Has 2 Potions of Healing (50gp each) 
in stock if characters can afford them. Can 
produce a number of differing sleep (DC13), 
paralysis (DC13) potions or weak poisons 
(1d6) if requested for the same price as the 
healing potions. 
Dendlin’s Goods (general store): This 
building looks to have been erected in the past 
Upon approach to the settlement of 
Lhuvenhead, late on the fourth afternoon. 
Grilop states that he will being staying in 
town to do some business for the next 
three nights. He obliges the characters 
remain around and out of trouble if they 
wish to continue to work for him. Grilop 
offers the party the promised 15gp each 
for their work and states that there will be 
twice the pay for the next leg of the 
journey if the characters continue to help. 
Grilop directs the cart to a small square 
within the village and begins to setup a 
market stand, yelling to the players to 
meet here on the morning he departs.  
Lhuvenhead itself is a quiet country style 
frontier settlement. Some houses lay in 
derelict wrecks around the area and close 
to the river a number of ruined docks and 
buildings can be seen stretching into the 
river. A small inn spills smoke from its 
single chimney near to the center green. 
Above the door lay a simple sign which 
reads “Orc’s End”. Around the inn a few 
buildings seem new and in order. It’s as if 
the town has recently rebuilt itself around 
a previous Catastrophe.

standard rates. Alternatively Lilt offers the 
party 5gp each for each set of orcish tusks

Completing either task should provide the 
party 75xp each. 
Hanging around the bar provides little 
information from the locals about current 
events. Eventually however on the last night 
characters remain in town, the party hears an 
old human named Junda discussing with a 
friend about one of the local rangers who 
became lost after being chased by 4 owlbears 
two days north west of town. After running for 
over an hour trying to avoid the vicious beasts 
the ranger stumbled across a set of huge stone 
heads in the forest. The heads appeared to be 
dwarvish, and wore strange helms. If 
questioned about any of this Junda states that 
the ranger in question has left for Everlund to 
deliver a set of skins and is named Fraid 
Kinglig.   
Lhuvenhead Surrounds 
The country side surrounding Lhuvenhead is 
mostly ancient pine forests which encircle the 
town to the west, north and east. Much further 
north the Nether Mountains can be seen 
reaching towards the heavens. To the south the 
River Rauven winds its way towards 
Silverymoon. Some in town suggest that a 
small orc encampment can be found through 
the forest to the north. It is agreed upon that all 
the surrounding forest is inhabited by a 
number of horrifying and deadly creatures, 
including bears, ogres and wolves.  The 
foothills of the mountains start some 8 miles to 
the north of town. If the characters decide to 
enter the woods outside of town they run the 
chances to encounter some form of trouble.  
Rolling a 1d4, odds will determine an 
encounter with consequences seen on the 
following table: 
Roll 
1d8 Consequences 
1-2 4 wolves attack the last character in the woods 
3-4 1 owlbear attacks from some small brush  
5-6  2 orcs roaming around for food 
7-8 1 brown bear attacks from the mouth of its cave 
(Creature Locations: Wolf MM pg.341, Owlbear MM pg.249, 
Orc pg.246 & Brown Bear pg.319)  
Each of these encounters should grant 25xp per 
character and 1 reward from the Individual 
Treasure: Challenge 0-4 Table from the DMG 
pg.136. 
Passing Time 
If characters have time they wish to pass while 
staying in Lhuvenhead, they can simply spend 
their time at the inn, practicing a profession or 
learning a new skill if practical.  
Before the party does leave however, the 
character in possession of the ornate dagger 
will have it disappear whilst they are 
sleeping/distracted on the final night before 
Grilop leaves.

Road to Everlund 
The archers view seems obscured as they have 
miss the party on every second attack 
regardless of rolls.  
Each of the two ambushers running towards 
Grilop are considered Thug’s (MM pg.350). 
The two firing arrows are considered Bandit’s 
(MM pg.343). Upon defeat players may notice 
that each of the attackers have a strange rat 
skull symbol on their leather armour. Any 
attacker left alive for questioning will be very 
resistant and will require a DC18 Charisma 
(Persuasion) Check to let the players know 
they have orders to kill Grilop from the Dead 
Rats. 
A DC17 Intelligence (History) Check will 
reveal this to be the symbol of the Dead Rats, 
a notorious thief’s guild usually operating out 
of Luskan. 
Upon the bodies of the attackers players find a 
total of 34gp and 23sp in coins. Additionally 
they find a contract letter with Grilop’s 
description and a dead or alive clause with a 
reward of 50gp. Characters who defeat the 
attackers also get 75xp each. 
Characters may ask Grilop explain this 
situation to them. Grilop will tell you all to 
“Shut up and if you mind your own business if 
you want an extra 5gp each” If pressed further 
Grilop will simply ignore the characters and 
continue to drive the cart onward to Everlund. 
A  Journey’s End 
As the party crests the final hill of the journey, 
Grilop halts the cart and gruffly thanks the 
party for their valiant efforts to keep himself 
and his business alive and well. He pays each 
character the agreed 35gp’s and ask’s the 
player’s not believe everything they hear. 
Grilop states he can be found in the merchant 
quarter if the party is more work and that for 
the party’s safety they should enter the city 
separately.  
 
  
After meeting a very hungover Grilop on 
the agreed upon morning, the merchant 
moans at the party to get a move on 
before urging the horses onward. The 
road leading toward Everlund is clearly 
the most travelled characters may have 
encountered thus far. As the party 
accompanies Grilop along the eight hour 
trek to Everlund they are met by an 
unusual sight. 
Four arrows stand driven partially on the 
road in a diamond shape. Atop each arrow 
is the head of a large rat. Grilop 
immediately looks fearful upon seeing 
this and begins yelling for players to 
withdraw their weapons and get ready to 
fight.  
Out of the bushes, arrows fly silently, 
slamming into the cart and ground near to 
the characters. Two roguish looking elves 
in dark leather armour spring from the 
bushes to either side of the cart and begin 
to lunge towards Grilop. Arrows continue 
to fly from behind the cart off the road to 
the east. Another two archers remain 
hidden firing their arrows upon the party 
from the safety of the trees. 
Stretched out westward, lay the large 
trading town of Everlund. As the sun’s 
final rays still light the towns many 
buildings, the party looks upon one of the 
largest cities in the entire region. Barges 
and watercraft flow along the River 
Rauvin, which Everlund hugs tightly for 
several hundred feet. People can be seen 
amongst the streets moving to and fro, a 
number of brightly lit taverns and inns 
can be seen with chimneys smoking.
