ADVENTURE PRIMER 
“Fanaticism requires extreme measures.” 
Bidderdoo Harpell, The Pirate King  
Pretty Teeth, Sharp Teeth is designed for three to seven 
1st-2nd-level characters and is optimized for five 
characters with an average party level (APL) of 1. 
Characters outside this level range cannot participate in 
this adventure. This adventure occurs in Longsaddle.  
Background 
A long time back, two squabbling cults took a shine to 
lakeside land near LONGSADDLE. Both sides followed 
MALAR, beast god of hunting and savagery. One bunch 
raised a chapel on the eastern bank, the other on the 
western. Well, that feud turned ugly in no time, and soon 
enough the land ran red with blood. THE HARPELLS, a 
family of eccentric wizards, transformed the worst 
offenders into RABBITS as a magical punishment, then 
banished them to a little island out in the lake. Though the 
original cultists are long gone, their bloodline lingers in 
these creatures. And wouldn’t you know it, didn’t take long 
for some fool to bring one of those rabbits back to the 
mainland. 
Most folks figure that story’s just a tale. But now and 
again, something stirs in the tall grass. A rabbit goes feral. 
Crops get torn up. Cattle scatter. Ranch hands come back 
with bite marks. Some of these rabbits are too big, too 
clever, and far too bold. 
That’s when the BLUE SIGIL got involved. A mercenary 
company made up of OGRES. One of their own, GERG, was 
hired by the local farmers to deal with the rabbit problem. 
However, the RABBIT FOLK are not without cunning. 
The most intelligent among them, HARENGON PRIESTS OF 
MALAR, have devised a plan to mislead a group of 
adventurers, painting the ogre as the villain and turning 
their enemies against one another. All the while, they wait 
in the tall grass, watching to finish off whoever wins. 
Overview 
The adventure begins with a Call to Action scene and has 
one main Part. The adventure takes approximately 1 hour 
to play. 
 
Call to Action. The characters are approached by a talking 
rabbit that seeks their help against an ogre. Agreeing to 
the task of dealing with the ogre is the Call to Action. 
Part One: Hunters. The characters journey into the plains 
and confront the ogre, learning that not everything is as it 
seems. Defeating either the Ogre or the Rabbits of Malar 
is the Story Objective. 
Adventure Hook 
Use these hooks to explain why the characters are in 
Longsaddle. 
Longriders. The character has heard that Longsaddle is a 
place where fortunes are made. Out on these frontier 
lands, ranchers, farmers, and wizards are always looking 
to hire folks who can fend off monsters, cattle thieves, 
and the occasional band of marauding barbarians. 
Longsaddler. The character is or was a resident of 
Longsaddle or the surrounding lands. They know the 
local faces, the Harpells’ reputation, and just how weird 
things can get around these parts. They might be just as 
strange themselves and have been accepted as part of the 
community all the same. 
Faction Agent. The character is working with one of the 
many factions of Faerûn, an adventuring society, or a 
guild. Either way, they are temporarily stationed in 
Longsaddle between missions, gathering information, 
maintaining a presence, or awaiting new orders. 
Harpell Apprentice. The character is currently staying at 
Ivy Mansion as part of an apprenticeship. Under the 
tutelage of the Harpells, they’ve been learning spells, 
dodging experiments gone wrong, and helping contain 
magical accidents. When the Harpells need someone to 
handle a strange task or investigate a disturbance, it’s 
usually the apprentices who get volunteered.

CALL TO ACTION 
Estimated Duration. 15 minutes 
 
The characters are on the road together, hired out of 
Longsaddle to deliver a wagonload of fence posts and dry 
goods to the Greely Ranch, two days south of the hamlet. It 
isn’t glamorous work, but Jed Greely promised 25 gold 
pieces upon delivery. Coin is coin, and the job also came 
with the promise of warm stew upon arrival. The merchant 
from Sixhorns Select Wares even lent them their mule to 
help with the hauling. Honey is a stubborn beast, no doubt 
about it, but easily persuaded with a carrot or two. 
Setting: Longsaddle 
The frontier hamlet of Longsaddle has always been a place 
where the strange is just plain common. Founded nigh on 
centuries ago, this small hamlet hugs a dusty stretch of the 
Long Road, catering to every rancher, farmer, and traveler 
passing through. It earned its fame thanks to the eccentric 
family of wizards known as the Harpells, who planted the 
Longsaddle square on the maps, whether the rest of Faerûn 
liked it or not. Tales of talking critters, dancing broomsticks, 
and spells gone sideways are just part of the scenery. Most 
folks just give a shrug and get on with their day.  
 Most of Longsaddle lies beyond the hamlet’s modest 
limits, scattered across the rolling plains in a patchwork of 
ranches and farms. Horses, longhorns, and sheep are 
common enough sights, but some ranchers raise dinosaurs 
instead of cattle, and a few even herd beasts so peculiar 
you’d swear they escaped from a wizard’s fever dream. 
Call to Action 
Agreeing to the task of dealing with the ogre is the Call to 
Action. 
Meeting Jack the Rabbit 
The characters are setting up camp for their first night 
beside the Long Road. Their wagon with Greely’s goods is 
pulled into a shallow gully for shelter, the mule is 
munching on grass, and the sky is just beginning to trade its 
daylight for stars. This is the first evening shared by the 
characters. After character introductions and roleplay 
interactions with one another, read aloud or paraphrase the 
following: 
The wind moves gentle across the plains, rustling dry grass and 
stirring the scent of dust and sage. The Long Road lies quiet 
behind the rise, no travelers in sight, just the slow creak from 
the wagon and the occasional snort from the mule. Then, from 
the tall grass, something moves. Slow. Careful. A shape too 
upright for a dog and too small for a man. It steps into the 
edge of your light, ears twitching, eyes wide and unblinking.  
It’s a rabbit. A big one. And then it speaks! 
“Pardon the intrusion, but might I trouble you for a moment 
of your time?” 
Area Information 
The plains have the following features. 
Terrain. The plains stretch wide and open, a sea of tall 
grasses dotted with wildflowers, sagebrush, and the 
occasional lone tree or rocky outcrop. Characters can see 
about 2 miles in any direction on a clear day, except 
where obstructions block their view. 
Lighting. During the day, the plains are bathed in bright 
light. A rocky outcrop or a lone tree provides shade, 
creating areas of dim light beneath them. 
Sounds. Wind rustling through grass is a near-constant 
presence.  
Weather. The plains are generally dry, with light winds. 
Daytime temperatures average around 72°F (22°C), while 
nights grow chilly, dropping to approximately 47°F (8°C). 
GREELY’S SUPPLIES 
The characters have been supplied by Sixhorns Select Wares, a 
sundries store that sold all manner of useful items, with a wagon 
with 30 cedar fence posts (each post weighs 35 lbs.) and 405 lbs. 
worth of dry goods (consisting of flour, oats, dried beans, lard, 
pickled vegetables, salted pork, sugar, soap, candles, and 
matches). These supplies, along with the wagon and the mule, 
Honey, are considered story items and may not be kept by the 
characters at the end of the adventure session. 
Creature Information 
Jack is a priest acolyte with the following characteristics: 
size Small, fey creature type, and knows the Deception skill. 
Jack has no gear and relies on natural armor for protection. 
If Jack needs to defend himself, he relies on his Radiant 
Flame ability.  
Jack hail the characters and ask for help, claiming that an 
evil ogre is trying to round up their family and devour them. 
He begs the party to slay the brute before more of their kin 
are lost. Jack claims they’re too small to stand against such 
brute strength. That’s why he has come seeking help. 
Using the Search action, a character who succeeds on a 
DC 15 Wisdom (Insight) check notices something strange 
about Jack’s expression. His eyes linger a little too long, 
watching the characters with an unsettling hunger. Still, 
there is something beneath their story that feels real. His 
words do not seem entirely false. 
If confronted about his staring, Jack tilts his head slightly 
and admits, with a soft chuckle, that he has not eaten yet. 
His gaze drifts toward the mule, just for a moment, before 
flickering to the wagon. He sniffs at the air, then politely 
inquires whether the party might be willing to spare a bit of 
food for a weary traveler.  
If the characters deny the request, Jack simply shrugs. If 
the characters choose to offer some of food, Jack accepts 
with a bow of thanks. He then tears into the meal with 
startling speed, devouring it in a manner that is anything 
but polite.

JACK 
Chaotic Evil Harengon (he/him) trickster  
Jack’s fur is a dusty gray, flecked with white, and his ears are long 
and expressive. The fey carries himself with a lazy confidence, 
slouching just enough to look harmless until he isn’t. 
What They Want. Jack wants the humanoids and the giant to 
fight one another. Whoever wins will be weakened enough for the 
Rabbits of Malar to finish off.  
Horrible with Names. Jack isn’t very good at remembering 
names, or maybe he just doesn’t care. He prefers to hand out 
offhand nicknames like “darlin,” “guv,” “sport,” or “chuckles.” He 
uses them whether they’re insulting or not. 
Development 
If the characters ask for payment in exchange for their help, 
Jack admits that his burrow may hold something of value. 
An old magical helmet left behind by a druid. Should they 
choose to help, the treasure will be theirs to claim. If the 
characters decide not to help or attack Jack, the Harengon 
retreats into the tall grass. The rabbit folk will attempt to 
attack the characters later the next day (see Adjusting the 
Adventure in Part 1).  
If the characters agree to help, Jack leads them through 
the plains to the ogre’s location, about three miles away. If 
they intend to travel tomorrow, Jack gives directions 
instead, urging them to hurry. He then vanishes into the 
grass. Proceed to Part 1.

PART 1: HUNTERS 
Estimated Duration. 40 minutes 
 
The ogre’s location is about three miles from the camp, just 
off the Long Road across open plains. If the characters 
choose to continue to Greely’s ranch, it takes a full day to 
reach it, and another day to backtrack to where Jack said 
the ogre was lurking. Once he leads the characters there, 
Jack vanishes into the tall grass. 
Story Objective 
Defeating either the Ogre or the Rabbits of Malar is the 
Story Objective. 
Meeting Gerg 
From a mile off, they spot a hulking figure crouched low in 
the tall grass near a patch of scrub and dirt. If the characters 
choose to approach, read or paraphrase the following:  
As you draw closer, it becomes clear it’s an ogre, hunched 
awkwardly over a hunting trap beside a rabbit burrow. His 
mounting frustration is plain as day. 
Creature Information 
Gerg (Chaotic Neutral, ogre) is clearly unsuited for delicate 
work. He’s part of the Blue Sigil, a mercenary company of 
ogres based out of Mog’s Keep near Longsaddle. Gerg hasn’t 
noticed the characters yet. If he does, he grunts and waves 
them off, wanting to be left alone. His starting attitude is 
Indifferent. In the surrounding brush are three hunting 
traps (including the one that Gerg is working on), though 
none of them are currently functional. A character 
proficient with tinker’s tools can repair a hunting trap with 
a successful DC 13 Intelligence check, taking about ten 
minutes per trap. Concealing each hunting trap requires a 
successful DC 14 Wisdom (Survival) check.  
GERG 
Chaotic Neutral Ogre (he/him) mercenary  
A towering ogre wearing a hemp-cloth overalls. The Blue Sigil 
tattoo, a blue circle crossed by a crescent moon and a lightning 
bolt, is branded on his left butt cheek, partially visible beneath the 
sagging fabric of his overalls. 
What They Want. Gerg wants to finish his job and return to 
Longsaddle to collect his reward.  
Talks Funny. Gerg speaks with a soft, rounded tone, replacing 
their R’s and L’s with W’s. Words like “really” become “weawwy,” 
and “look out, rabbit!” becomes “wook out, wabbit!”  
 
Characters who study the brand on the ogre’s butt can take 
the Study action and make a DC 10 Intelligence (History) 
check to recognize the symbol. The Blue Sigil is a mercenary 
company of ogres, formed after their tribe decided to fight 
back against constant adventurer raids. Now operating 
across the North, they’ve earned a reputation for reliability. 
Development 
The characters may choose to attack Gerg. The ogre defends 
himself as best he can, aiming to knock the characters 
unconscious rather than kill them. If slain, after one hour, 
the rabbits emerge from their burrow to see what 
happened. Upon finding the ogre dead, they seem delighted. 
They slip out in great numbers to dance in a circle around 
the characters… before launching a sudden assault. If the 
characters have already moved on from the area, the 
rabbits hunt after the characters. See When Rabbits 
Attack. 
ADJUSTING THE SCENE  
Here are some suggestions for adjusting this scene: 
 
• Weak. Gerg is an ogrillion ogre that starts with 34 hit points.   
• Average. Gerg starts with 34 hit points.  
• Strong. Gerg has his standard hit point total.  
If the characters ask why the ogre is hunting the rabbits, 
read or paraphrase the following: 
The ogre squints at you with heavy-lidded eyes, scratching his 
belly as he speaks in a soft, oddly gentle voice. “Wanchers 
‘wound Wongsaddwe paid me to smash wabbits,” he says. 
“Dey’s causin’ twoubwe. Scawin’ da cows. I smash, dey pay. 
That’s the deaw.” 
Gerg basically explains he was hired by farmers near 
Longsaddle. The rabbits have been attacking livestock, 
ruining crops, and stealing eggs from chicken rookeries. A 
few cattle even vanished. The farmers offered coin to the 
Blue Sigil and permission to smash whatever he found. That 
was good enough for Gerg.  
If the characters choose to help the ogre, Gerg suggests 
making camp near the rabbit burrow and waiting for the 
creatures to strike after dark. If any hunting traps have 
been repaired and successfully concealed, one rabbit of 
malar is caught in each trap when the rabbits attack that 
night.  
Optionally, the characters can enter the burrow (Area 
R1). Gerg is too large to fit inside and remains nearby. 
Characters who take the time to explore the surrounding 
area may discover a second entrance to the burrow (Area 
R2), hidden beneath a tangle of roots and grass. Finding it 
requires a successful DC 15 Intelligence (Investigation) 
check.

Area Information 
The Rabbit Burrow has the following features: 
Dimensions and Terrain. The tunnels are made of packed 
dirt, roughly 3 feet high and 3 feet wide, just large enough 
for a Medium creature to crawl on hands and knees. 
Movement for Medium creatures is treated as difficult 
terrain. Small creatures can move through the tunnels 
with little difficulty. 
Lighting. There are no sources of light within the burrow, 
except for the magic helm in area R4.  
Sounds. The burrow walls muffle most sound from the 
outside. Periodically there are unsettling rabbit shrieks 
that echo from deeper in the warren. 
Alert. If the sound of combat breaks out anywhere within 
the burrow, all nearby inhabitants converge on the 
source of the sound and join the fight as quickly as 
possible. 
Entrances (R1 and R2). These are the primary and 
secondary access points to the burrow from the surface. 
Rabbit Nests (R3). Several nests made of fur, grass, and 
gnawed bones are tucked into the walls of the tunnels. At 
your option, a nest is occupied or vacant (see When 
Rabbits Attack).  
Shrine to Malar (R4). Deep within the warren, the walls 
are smeared with muddy pawprints and streaks of red. 
The shrine itself is fashioned from matted tufts of fur, 
splintered bones, and gnawed antlers arranged into a 
rough altar. A claw-shaped symbol is etched into the dirt 
floor. A successful DC 13 Intelligence (Religion) check 
identifies it as the symbol of Malar, god of the hunt, 
bloodlust, and bestial savagery. The shrine chamber has a 
10-foot ceiling.  
HOLIDAY CONNECTIONS 
If you’d like, you can add the following detail to Area R4. Nestled 
in woven grass baskets at the foot of the shrine are 2d4 brightly 
dyed eggs. Offerings to Malar left by the rabbit folk. Characters 
can identify them as mundane chicken eggs.  
Why are there eggs here? Malarite mumbo jumbo. 
Treasure 
At the foot of the shrine rests a glowing steel helm with a 
polished bronze hue, crowned with curving bull horns. This 
is a Dread Helm imbued with the Beacon minor property. 
When Rabbits Attack 
If the characters defeat Gerg, crawl into the rabbit burrow, 
or wait until nightfall with Gerg, the rabbit folk launch an 
attack. The rabbits of malar use the giant badger stat 
block with the following adjustments: Size Small. 
The harengon priest uses the priest acolyte stat block 
with these adjustments: Size Small, fey creature type, and 
proficiency in the Deception skill. They have no chain shirt 
and mace and rely on natural armor for protection. Their 
alignment is Chaotic Evil. The harengon priest rely on their 
Radiant Flame for attacking. The swarm of rabbits use the 
swarm of rats stat block.  
If Jack is still alive, he leads the assault. 
For Strong groups, replace harengon priest with a 
harengon druid with these adjustments: Size Small, fey 
creature type, and proficiency in the Deception skill. They 
have no studded leather and rely on natural armor for 
protection. Their alignment is Chaotic Evil. The Harengon 
Druid rely on their Verdant Wisp and Spellcasting for 
attacking. 
ADJUSTING THE SCENE 
Here are some suggestions for adjusting this scene: 
 
• Very Weak. Remove a rabbit of malar 
• Weak. Remove a swarm of rabbits 
• Average. A harengon priest, two rabbits of malar, and a 
swarms of rabbit 
• Strong. Replace harengon priest with harengon druid  
• Very Strong. Add two rabbits of malar 
ADJUSTING THE ADVENTURE 
It’s entirely possible the characters decide not to help Jack, ignore 
the ogre situation, and focus on doing their job. Delivering 
Greely’s goods before heading back to Longsaddle. If that’s the 
case, the Rabbits of Malar aren’t quite done with them. During the 
journey to the ranch or on the return trip, the rabbits launch an 
ambush, striking from the tall grass. If the characters choose to 
follow the tracks after the battle, a successful DC 12 Wisdom 
(Survival) check leads them back to the rabbit burrow. By this 
time, Gerg is already dead. His carcass has been dragged into the 
burrow and now rests in Area R4. Only a few rabbit folk are left 
behind.  
 If there is not enough time for the adventure session, then all 
the rabbit folk have fled from the burrow.  
ADJUSTING THE SCENE  
Here are some suggestions for the few rabbit folk left behind: 
 
• Average. A harengon priest and swarm of 
rabbits  
• Strong. Replace harengon priest with harengon druid 
CLERICS AND ACOLYTES OF MALAR 
If one of the characters is a known devotee of Malar, the rabbit 
folk may view the character as a traitor to the faith. In this case, 
they focus their fury on that character.  
Development 
Once the rabbits are defeated, the characters can explore 
the burrow and recover the treasure in Area R4. If Gerg is 
still alive, the ogre thanks them and begins collecting rabbit 
carcasses as proof for the farmers of Longsaddle. If the 
characters ask for a share of the bounty, Gerg explains that 
the contract was made between his handlers in the Blue 
Sigil and the farmers. He doesn’t have a say in how the 
reward is divided. Still, he invites the party to meet him 
later at the Gilded Horseshoe, a local inn, and promises to 
treat them to the best breakfast in all the North. Once the 
characters are ready to return to Longsaddle or complete 
their delivery of Greely’s supplies, proceed to Conclusion.

CONCLUSION 
Estimated Duration. 5 minutes 
 
This adventure has a few different endings, depending on 
the choices the characters made along the way. 
 
• If the characters sided with Gerg and helped him defeat 
the Rabbits of Malar, they return to Longsaddle to a 
warm welcome. See Meeting Up with Gerg and the 
Farmers of Longsaddle Ending.  
• If Gerg was slain but the Rabbits of Malar were 
destroyed, the people of Longsaddle still offer 
gratitude for the party’s actions. See the Farmers of 
Longsaddle Ending.  
• If the characters chased off Jack during the Call to 
Action, chose to deliver Greely’s goods instead, and 
never ventured to the rabbit burrow, see You Won’t 
Believe What Happened Ending.  
• If the characters slew Gerg, well… see This Means War 
Ending.  
Meeting Up with Gerg 
Back in Longsaddle, the characters make their way to the 
Gilded Horseshoe . True to his word, Gerg is waiting for 
them. Gerg is cleaned up as best he can be and already 
working his way through a platter of smoked sausage. He 
waves the characters over with a grin and treats them to a 
hearty breakfast, all paid in full. Between mouthfuls, he 
raises a mug and toasts their help, calling them “the best 
wabbit-smashers this side o’ the Spine.” The characters 
earn the Story Award: Ogre Friend.  
The Farmers of Longsaddle 
Now word don’t take long to travel around, folks in 
Longsaddle know what the characters done out on them 
wild plains. The farmers of Longsaddle can’t pay the bounty 
for the rabbits directly, due to their contract with the Blue 
Sigil mercenary group. They’re not keen on upsetting an 
entire company of ogres.  
However, they offer another form of thanks for the 
characters role in clearing out the nest of predatory 
varmints. The characters are each presented with a token of 
local gratitude: hand-stitched embroidered bandana 
trinkets, a basket of fresh baked goods and preserves, and, 
most notably, a weapon from local legend. 
The farmers gather to present the group with a magic 
spear once wielded by a retired adventurer turned goat 
herder named Zigfreed. Zigfreed claimed he was the best 
dragon-slayer this side of the Spine of the World. Spent his 
final years watching over his land with naught but his goats, 
a jug of moonshine, and that spear by his side. The farmers 
reckon the characters can do more with it. Zigfreed’s Spear 
is a Sylvan Talon imbued with the Songcraft minor property. 
You Won’t Believe What Happened 
Now when the characters mosey on back into Longsaddle, 
they find the hamlet’s already stirring with talk of trouble. 
Folks whisper about rabbits attacking farms and their 
livestock. Turns out the farmers hired Gerg, that ogre from 
the Blue Sigil mercenary company, to handle the mess. But 
wouldn’t you know it, things went sideways. The poor ogre 
wound up dead, and when the Longsaddlers went to check 
the burrow, there wasn’t a hare left in sight. Just an empty 
warren and a nasty little shrine to Malar, the god of blood, 
fang, and the hunt. Folks reckon them Rabbits of Malar are 
still out there.  
This Means War 
In time, the Blue Sigil mercenaries piece together what 
really happened. After interrogating a captured Malarite 
rabbit, they learn the truth. The characters killed Gerg, one 
of their own. The characters acquire the Story Award: Blue 
Sigil Feud. The ogres of the North don’t forget a slight, and 
they don’t forgive one, either.

REWARDS 
At the end of the session, the characters receive rewards based upon their accomplishments. 
CHARACTER REWARDS 
The characters earn the following rewards: 
Advancement 
A character participating in this adventure gains one level.  
Gold 
If found during the adventure the following items are 
converted to gold and added to any coins found during the 
adventure. The gold is split among the party members. If 
someone wants to keep an item in particular that character 
can either take it as part of their share, or pay for it with 
their own gold. Mundane items are not duplicated nor 
included in this.  
Item Gold Value 
Greely’s Reward 25 gp 
Maximum 25 gp 
 
Magic Item(s) 
If found during the adventure, the characters can keep the 
following magic items; these items are described in 
Appendix 2: 
• Dread Helm 
• Sylvan Talon (Spear) 
Story Awards(s) 
• Blue Sigil Feud 
• Ogre Friend 
 
These awards are described in Appendix 2.
