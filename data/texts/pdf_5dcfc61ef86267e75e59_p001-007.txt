 To entice my players into the hidden Witchlight 
Carnival beer garden, I initiate the “MYSTERY MINE” 
encounter in the first chapter of “The Wild Beyond the 
Witchlight.” If you, as the Dungeon Master, are eager to 
guide your players to the beer garden, you might introduce 
a rumor about free beer being offered at the Mystery 
Mine attraction. Zephixo, the Witchlight hand in charge 
of ticketing for the attraction, plays a pivotal role here. If 
players inquire about the free beer, Zephixo proposes a deal: 
they can enter the Mystery Mine without having their tickets 
punched, but only if they agree to peer into his “All-Seeing 
Eye” device.
 Should a character choose to look into the “All-
Seeing Eye,” it triggers an illusion involving the creature they 
fear most, which then appears during the dragon encounter 
(instead of the dragon) detailed later in this text. If a PC looks 
into the eye, slip a pm or folded up piece of paper to your 
player asking them to write down\type what monster their 
PC fears the most. Either way the PC’s will eventually decide 
to board the ride, with a maximum of two medium-sized 
characters per cart, you can advance the narrative by reading 
“Welcome To The Party” aloud to your players.

 As you approach the lively festivities, you notice 
workers busily tending to a nearby brewery. They move 
with purpose, transporting keg barrels from a massive vat 
to a wagon nearby. One worker briefly pauses to give you a 
nod before resuming his duties. The scene unfolds into an 
avenue filled with celebration, marked by evident indulgence 
in drinking.
 Present the map to the players. The laborers, while 
amiable, are keen to return to their tasks after sharing a 
slight smile. However, if questioned, they offer a word of 
caution before departing: they advise the party to steer clear 
of the dwarven spirits, warning, “That stuff will put you 
into next week!”
#2 Mithezisk Brandorbrook
 Off in the corner of the festivities is a brawny 
man covered in white fur with the head of a horse! 
The hair upon his head is down to his shoulders and 
quite human like even though it is a myriad of different 
bright colors. He has a large yellow unicorn like horn 
in his mane that knocks into a barrel with each rep. 
You see, the unicorn man is currently laying with his 
back upon the ground while bench pressing a full 
barrel of ale! A swarm of lady pixies cheer him on and 
count out loud in high pitched voices with each rep. 
“5.......6.......7.......8!” and when the unicorn man gets 
to ten he sets the barrel off to the side, leaps up to 
his feet and flexes his muscles to the delight of his 
small audience.
 
 Mithezisk offers up free mini pints of his latest 
concoction, a strong dark ale that leaves you with whiskey 
breath, he calls “Breakfast Beer”. At all times the swarm of 7 
pixie ladies flutter around him and giggle. Mithezisk does his 
best to win the parties vote over with his brew and for a full 
gold piece he will have a pixie sprinkle a bit of sparkling pixie 
dust into a fresh pint but will only offer up this delicacy to a 
party member who can lift a keg of ale over their head and 
win the pixies over. (Athletics DC 18).
 If a player ingests the pixie dust breakfast beer they 
gain a flying speed of 30 feet for one minute. If effect ends 
while in mid flight the PC feather falls safely to the ground. 
If the PC attempts to fly off the map they will disappear into 
the fog and immediately reappear as they fly back into the 
festival through the main entrance (#0).
#3 Dorzes Brandorbrook
 As you approach, you notice a rugged, bulky man 
behind the table, diligently wiping down tankards with a 
mostly clean rag. The air is filled with the scent of old, stale 
ale, intertwined with a sweet, candy-like aroma. “Care for a 
free taste?” he asks in a half-hearted grumble.
 Dorzes carries a heavy heart, secretly in love with 
Glorianna, a half-elf maiden who travels with the festival. 
She captivates the drunk crowds with her music but remains 
oblivious to Dorzes’s affection. Unfortunately for Dorzes, 
Glorianna has her eyes set on his brother, Mithezisk, the 
unicorn man, who merely sees her as a casual fling.
 Dorzes’s signature “Liquorice Root Ale” is a very 
dark black brew that absorbs rather than reflects light. Its 
taste, reminiscent of absinthe, is intense and not easily 
palatable for everyone. Despite this, Dorzes generously offers 
free samples to passersby. For those who acquire the taste, a 
full tankard is available for 3 copper pieces, and 5-liter mini 
kegs can be purchased for 3 silver pieces.
#4 Lamilda’s Masks
 Lamilda, an elderly woman with the vibrant attire 
of a belly dancer, dedicates much of her time to crafting 
ornate masquerade masks. She enjoys a close friendship with 
Silda, the benevolent matriarch of the beer festival, who has 
provided her with a space to showcase her artistic creations. 
Her masks, some adorned with small gemstones, vary in 
price from 1 to 500 gold pieces.
 Recognizing the challenge of making a sustainable 
living from her art alone, Lamilda also sells fresh fish and 
exotic throw rugs with Eladrin designs from the Feywild. 
Her lively and engaging personality not only adds charm 
to her stall but also proves to be an effective tool in her 
salesmanship.

 This tent is a haven for brewing enthusiasts,

#7 Main Bar
 At the bar, a lively crowd cheers on several 
women engaged in a spirited ale-drinking race. The 
chant of “Chug, chug, chug!” fills the air. As you draw 
closer to the bar, one of the contestants, mid-chug, 
passes out, tumbling off her stool to land prone 
at your feet!
 The unconscious competitor, Sallyia, is a middle-
aged human rogue with red hair, slim features, and, 
curiously, a real set of butterfly wings sprouting from 
her back. She snores loudly until someone comes to her 
aid. Thankfully, she hasn’t injured her head and regains 
consciousness if a party member or another patron 
successfully performs a DC 10 Medicine check.
 As Sallyia finds her feet again, another contestant 
bows out of the competition, and a winner emerges: a 
slender, bearded female dwarf sporting a crooked eyepatch. 
Celebrating her victory, she lets out a belch and proposes 
a conga line, turning the bar patrons into a spontaneous 
dance mob. Energized by bardic music, they form a line 
and start a merry procession around the beer festival, even 
incorporating limbo into their revelry.
 Larth Brandorbrook, the main bartender, is always 
ready to serve his specialty, “Blonde Girl” ale. He often quips 
that “it goes down as easy as a light beer”! Larth prefers 
brewing lighter ales, reasoning that customers can drink 
more compared to darker ales, thus potentially increasing 
sales at 2 copper pieces per tankard.
 The brew-off ballot box is prominently located 
at the main bar. It’s a large, circular goldfish bowl where 
patrons slip in folded parchment pieces to cast their votes 
for the best beer of the Witchlight Beer Festival. Larth 
readily supplies parchment, ink, and quill to those who need 
them for voting.

 You’re reveling in the beer garden, surrounded by 
friends, jubilant music, and lively drinking contests, when 
suddenly a fearsome roar cuts through the festive air. A large, 
dark shadow moves ominously through the fog overhead, 
sending a wave of unease through the crowd. Then, a 
massive red-scaled dragon bursts forth from the low-hanging 
clouds, perching atop a building and drawing a deep breath, 
preparing to unleash fire. The festival erupts into chaos, 
resembling a disturbed anthill. This is the cue for everyone to 
roll initiative.
As the encounter unfolds, have the players roll initiative 
and follow the turn order. With each completed round, an 
illusion of a creature feared by one of the PCs, as previously 
stated into Zephixo’s “All-Seeing Eye Contraption,” 
materializes to join the dragon. If no fears were disclosed, 
only the dragon appears. The illusions have an AC of 12, 
Initiative +0, and on each of their turns, the dragon uses 
its illusory breath weapon (Dex Save DC 15). A failed save 
results in the flames harmlessly washing over the target, 
causing no actual damage. The illusionary creatures have 
an attack bonus of +5 but deal no damage on a hit as their 
attacks pass right through their targets like a ghost. Despite 
attacks seemingly passing through the dragon and other 
illusions, the illusions dissipate after three successful hits. 
If a PC succeeds on an Arcana check (DC 13) during the 
encounter, they realize the creatures are merely illusions. 
Any beer festivals patrons that get caught up in the harmless 
flames are actually illusions there to set the stage. No actual 
man, woman, or child gets injured during the chaos for it is 
all staged. 
 The atmosphere rapidly transforms from chaos to 
jubilation as the festival attendees give three hearty cheers 
to the heroes for their valiant “defeat” of the dragon! For 
the rest of the celebration, the player characters (PCs) are 
lauded like renowned bards. The enthusiastic audience insists 
on them taking the stage for a musical performance. Each 
party member should make a Performance check (DC 12). If 
at least half of the party succeeds, they surprisingly deliver 
a respectable performance. Even in the case of failure, the 
crowd continues to support them with fervent cheers.
 The excitement escalates when a unicorn emerges 
from a burst of fog, a trophy dangling from its horn. The 
trophy, a brass cup, quickly becomes a cherished vessel, 
constantly refilled with beer by the festival-goers. However, 
as the unicorn presents the trophy, a group of goblins 
ambushes the creature, attempting to saw off its horn. The 
number of goblins matches the number of PCs in the group.
 After dealing with the assailants, the celebration 
resumes unabated with music, dancing, beer, and a conga 
line. The usual voting for the best beer is bypassed as the 
PCs are once again brought onstage to announce their own 
personal favorite brew of the festival. The beer that they 
collectively choose wins, and its brewer is honored as the 
brew master. The victorious brewer steps forward to receive 
a decorative wreath with a central plaque, which Silda 
prepares to hand over for the presentation. The party at the 
Witchlight Beer Festival continues into the evening, 
under a perpetually foggy twilight sky that radiates 
nothing but delight.
 When the party concludes, and the heroes can 
no longer stand from the festivities, they decide to leave 
the festival. In a delightful send-off, they are crowd-surfed 
through the mist (#0) and then find themselves back in a 
mining cart. The cart rattles down the track, exiting the 
cave, and returns them to where their adventure began: the 
Mystery Mine attraction within the Witchlight Carnival.
