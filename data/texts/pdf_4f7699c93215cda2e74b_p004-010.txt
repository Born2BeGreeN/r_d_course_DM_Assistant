FR-DC-SCROG-02 Bad Weather Over Longsaddle (v1.4)       3 
 
ADVENTURE PRIMER 
"We dragons pride ourselves on grace and 
majesty… except that one. The flying stink barrel. 
He’s an embarrassment to the species." 
Saryndalaghlothtor, “Lady Gemcloak” 
Bad Weather Over Longsaddle is designed for three 
to seven 1st-2nd-level characters and is optimized for 
five characters with an average party level (APL) of 1. 
Characters outside this level range cannot participate in 
this adventure. This adventure occurs in Longsaddle.  
Background 
The hardy folk of LONGSADDLE have had about all they 
can stomach. See, for weeks now, a mischievous copper 
dragon - goes by the name of OL’ STINKYBUTT to 
anyone who's had the misfortune of looking up at the 
wrong time - has been flying over the farms, 
bombarding homesteads with something a whole lot 
worse than fire and brimstone. DRAGON POOP.  
 Roofs have been busted, wells fouled, and more than 
a few poor longhorns and ranch hands ended up 
injured. When the folk of Longsaddle went complaining 
to THE HARPELLS, the hamlet’s long-standing family of 
eccentric wizards, the wizards answered with what can 
only be described as a collective shrug. Busy with 
arcane experiments and none too keen on tromping 
after dragons, they decided the problem was better 
solved by handing it off to a few adventurers who could 
face down the dragon and set things right again. 
Overview 
The adventure begins with a Call to Action scene and 
has one main Part. The adventure takes approximately 
1 hour to play. 
Call to Action. As the characters head northwest, 
following rumors of dragon activity, they come across 
the aftermath of a recent flyover. Following the trail 
of destruction is the Call to Action. 
Part 1. Wranglers. The characters encounter the 
copper dragon up to their old tricks. Convincing Ol’ 
Stinkybutt to stop defecating on the properties, 
people, and beasts of Longsaddle is the Story 
Objective. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Adventure Hook  
Use these hooks to explain why the characters are in 
Longsaddle. 
Longrider. The character has heard that Longsaddle is a 
place where fortunes are made. Out on these frontier 
lands, ranchers, farmers, and wizards are always 
looking to hire folks who can fend off monsters, cattle 
thieves, and the occasional band of marauding 
barbarians. 
Longsaddler. The character is or was a resident of 
Longsaddle or the surrounding lands. They know the 
local faces, the Harpells’ reputation, and just how 
weird things can get around these parts. They might 
be just as strange themselves and have been accepted 
as part of the community all the same. 
Faction Agent. The character is working with one of the 
many factions of Faerûn, an adventuring society, or a 
guild. Either way, they are temporarily stationed in 
Longsaddle between missions, gathering information, 
maintaining a presence, or awaiting new orders. 
Harpell Apprentice. The character is currently staying 
at Ivy Mansion as part of an apprenticeship. Under 
the tutelage of the Harpells, they’ve been learning 
spells, dodging experiments gone wrong, and helping 
contain magical accidents. When the Harpells need 
someone to handle a strange task or investigate a 
disturbance, it’s usually the apprentices who get 
volunteered.

CALL TO ACTION 
Estimated Duration. 15 minutes 
 
The characters have been hired out of Longsaddle to 
look into a local nuisance, a copper dragon that goes by 
the name Ol’ Stinkybutt. He’s been flying over the 
ranchlands, dropping foul surprises on roofs, wells, and 
anyone unlucky enough to be caught outside. The 
Harpells want the party to tell him to knock it off, 
preferably without undue violence, and have promised 
a reward through the family’s discretionary reward 
system. That is wizard talk for "we will give you a magic 
item," though they have not exactly said what kind. Still, 
a bit of magic in the pouch never hurts in this line of 
work.  
 To help the characters, the Harpells have even 
provided a gift for the dragon, or as some folks call it, a 
bribe. It is an enormous, lumpy statue of a longhorn cow 
with one horn longer than the other and eyes that do 
not quite point in the same direction. Hauling the ugly 
thing is a wooden cart hitched to Honey (mule), a 
shaggy, unimpressed-looking mule. The Harpells also 
provide each character with a riding horse (for 
medium-size characters) or a mastiff (for small-size 
characters) with accompanying riding saddles. The 
mastiffs resemble giant Welsh corgis. 
 Based on recent sightings, the Harpells believe Ol’ 
Stinkybutt is a young dragon and reckon the characters 
are most likely to find him by patrolling the ranchlands 
northwest of the hamlet.  
Call to Action 
Following the trail of destruction is the Call to Action. 
That’s One Big Pile of Dung 
The characters begin their patrol the moment they 
depart the frontier hamlet of Longsaddle, heading 
northwest toward the ranchlands where Ol’ Stinkybutt 
has been most active. The plains stretch out in every 
direction, a sea of golden grasses rippling in the wind. 
Scattered buttes rise like sunbaked sentinels from the 
flatland. Here and there, sagebrush clings to the cracked 
earth, and narrow game trails wind between patches of 
scrub and dry creek beds. On clear days, the jagged 
peaks of the Crag Mountains loom far to the north.  
 On the third day of traveling the plains, the characters 
come across the aftermath of a recent flyover. Read 
aloud or paraphrase the following: 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Area Information 
This stretch of the plains have the following features. 
Terrain. The plains stretch wide and open, a sea of tall 
grasses dotted with wildflowers, sagebrush, and the 
occasional lone tree or rocky outcrop. Characters can 
see about 2 miles in any direction on a clear day, 
except where obstructions block their view. 
Lighting. During the day, the plains are bathed in bright 
light. A rocky outcrop or a lone tree provides shade, 
creating areas of dim light beneath them. 
Sounds. Wind rustling through grass is a near-constant 
presence.  
Weather. The plains are generally dry, with light winds. 
Daytime temperatures average around 72°F (22°C), 
while nights grow chilly, dropping to approximately 
47°F (8°C). 
Tracks. A character can use the Search action to inspect 
the tracks left behind by Lee’s longhorns. On a DC 10 
Wisdom (Survival) check, they see clear signs that a 
herd of longhorns passed through the area 
approximately four hours ago. Their hoofprints are 
scattered and erratic, suggesting they were spooked 
and fled in a panic. They fled north.  
Dragon Dung. A steaming pile of dragon dung, roughly 
the size of a hay bale. A character can use the Study 
action to inspect the pile of dung left behind by Ol’ 
Stinkybutt. If they succeed on a DC 15 Intelligence 
(Nature) check, they learn that the dung is mildly 
acidic and unfit for fertilizer. Its corrosive properties 
are too weak to be used as a weapon in its raw form.   
THE HARPELLS’ GIFT 
The characters are delivering a 400 lbs. stone statue of a 
poorly crafted longhorn cow, hauled by cart and a mule 
named Honey. Each player character is also provided with a 
mount and saddle. The statue, cart, mounts, riding saddles, 
and mule are story items and may not be kept after the 
adventure. Pay attention to what the characters do with the 
cart and statue in Part One, Encounter A.  
Creature Information 
Buried in the dragon dung is an unfortunate human  
ranch hand struggling to climb out of the muck. Lee  
(scout) is currently at 2 hit points due to a busted hip  
from falling off their horse. Lee has the prone and  
restrained condition and two levels of exhaustion. A  
character can pull her free with a DC 10 Strength  
(Athletics) check. On a success, Lee is freed and  
immediately collapses to catch her breath. 
 Lee explains she was watching Mister Zelorrgosz’s 
longhorns when something struck her and knocked her 
off her horse. Before she could react, she found herself 
buried in dragon dung. As she scans the area, panic sets 
in when she realizes the herd is gone. With rising 
urgency, she begs the characters to help round up the 
lost longhorns.  If the characters ask about a reward, 
Lee promises she can pay them in coin. She explains 
that she had been saving up to visit the Gambling 
Golem, but has no problem parting with the gold if it 
means keeping her job.  
A foul wind hits you first. Just over the next rise, you find 
the source: a steaming mound of dung the size of a hay 
bale. It’s fresh. Swarming with flies. Just as you’re taking 
in the sheer scale, and smell, of the mess, a wet squelch 
breaks the silence. The dung pile shifts. Then again. A 
hand, coated elbow-deep in filth, reaches out and grabs 
the grass. “Help.. urk.. somebody help me!”

LEE 
Neutral Human (she/her) unfortunate rancher 
Lee is a lanky teenager with sunburned skin and a mess of 
straw-colored hair. She wears a too-big canvas duster over 
patched overalls and scuffed boots, all of which are currently 
caked in dragon dung. 
What They Want. Lee just wants to round up her runaway 
longhorns, get them back to Zelorrgosz’s ranch before 
sundown, and take a bath. 
 Rustic Drawl. Lee speaks in a slow, plainspoken cowboy 
drawl and light on punctuation. She uses phrases like 
“reckon,” “ain’t right,” and “more trouble than a rattlesnake 
in a washbasin.” Lee refers to Ol’ Stinkybutt only as “that 
airborne menace.” 
 Lasso Maker. Lee is an avid lasso enthusiast who spends 
her spare time crafting lassos by hand while keeping watch 
over Zelorrgosz’s cattle. 
Not too far from the scene is Lucky (riding horse), 
Lee’s horse, grazing on the grass. Either on her own or 
with the characters’ help, Lee limps over and rummages 
through Lucky’s saddlebags. She pulls out a set of five 
lassos and hands them out to the characters. As she 
distributes them, she offers a few quick tips on their 
use: success depends more on timing and control than 
raw strength. Once the loop lands, the wrangler must 
pull the rope taut and hold firm to guide the animal. 
 Once the characters are ready to move on, proceed to 
Part 1.  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
LASSO 
A lasso is a loop of rope designed to be thrown around a 
target and pulled tight to catch or restrain it. Commonly used 
by ranchers, it's a practical tool for wrangling cattle or other 
large animals. Treat each lasso as a reskinned Net.  
NET     
Adventuring Gear 
Weight: 3 lb. Cost: 1 GP 
When you take the Attack action, you can replace one of your 
attacks with throwing a Net. Target a creature you can see 
within 15 feet of yourself. The target must succeed on a 
Dexterity saving throw (DC 8 plus your Dexterity modifier and 
Proficiency Bonus) or have the Restrained condition until it 
escapes. The target succeeds automatically if it is Huge or 
larger. 
 To escape, the target or a creature within 5 feet of it must 
take an action to make a DC 10 Strength (Athletics) check, 
freeing the Restrained creature on a success. Destroying the 
Net (AC 10; 5 HP; Immunity to Bludgeoning, Poison, and 
Psychic damage) also frees the target, ending the effect. 
Source: Player’s Handbook (2024), page 227. Available in the 
SRD 5.2.1 and the Basic Rules (2024).

PART 1: WRANGLERS 
Estimated Duration. 45 minutes 
 
It takes about an hour of travel before the characters 
spot a herd of longhorns grazing in the distance, roughly 
two miles ahead. From their vantage point, they can 
clearly make out a large copper dragon, about the size 
of a lanky warhorse, soaring high above the plains. The 
dragon circles lazily above the herd like a bird of prey. 
When the characters close to within a mile, the dragon 
climbs higher into the sky and vanishes into the clouds. 
Story Objective 
Convincing Ol’ Stinkybutt to stop defecating on the 
properties, people, and beasts of Longsaddle is the 
Story Objective. 
Encounter A. Time to Wrangle 
It takes approximately 15 minutes for the party to reach 
the area. Read aloud or paraphrase the following: 
Up ahead, a small herd of cattle grazes peacefully.  
As you approach, the herd suddenly bolts, bellowing and 
crashing into one another in panic. A moment later, the 
cause becomes clear. A pack of jackals bursts from the tall 
grass and fans out behind the cattle, barking and yipping 
as they nip at the herd's flanks. 
Area Information 
The plains share the same general features outlined in 
the Call to Action, though without the presence of 
dragon dung. 
Creature Information 
There are a number of longhorns (ox) equal to the 
number of player characters. Each longhorn begins the 
encounter with a Hostile attitude and starts 40 feet 
away from the characters, fleeing across the plains in 
the same direction. The longhorns are being pursued by 
two jackalweres in jackal form, who are focused on 
driving one of the cattle away from the herd. The 
jackalwere begin the encounter with a Hostile attitude 
and start 20 feet away from the longhorns.  
  Unbeknownst to the characters, these jackalweres 
are members of the Gogan Brothers Gang, a fiendish 
family of notorious outlaws responsible for rustling, 
thefts, and scams along The Long Road. The jackalweres 
are not initially interested in the characters and attempt 
to complete their hunt. If the characters interfere, the 
jackalweres defend themselves but will attempt to flee 
if reduced to the bloodied condition. The Gogan siblings 
constantly bicker among themselves, blaming each 
other for any setbacks and fumbling through makeshift 
strategies. If they flee, they split up in different 
directions to make pursuit more difficult. 
ADJUSTING THE SCENE  
Here are some suggestions for adjusting this scene: 
 
• Very Weak. Replace one jackalwere with two jackals 
• Average. Two jackalweres. Longhorns equal to the number 
of player characters 
• Strong. Add a jackalwere 
• Very Strong. Add a jackalwere  
Wilderness Chase 
Use map 2.2 as the battle map for this encounter and 
map 2.3 as the chase tracker (see Appendix 1). The 
longhorns are the targets of the chase and remain at the 
0 marker. The jackalweres begin at the -20 ft. marker, 
and the characters start at the -40 ft. marker. All 
movement is tracked relative to the longhorns, who are 
always treated as staying at +0 for simplicity. When the 
longhorns move, all other participants fall back by the 
same distance. For example, if the longhorns move 40 
feet, all pursuers shift 40 feet farther from the 0 marker. 
At initiative count 0 each round, any longhorn not 
within line of sight of either the characters or the jackals 
makes a Dexterity (Stealth) check with disadvantage.  
On the open plains, longhorns can disappear from view 
behind rocky outcrops, in dry creek beds, through tall 
prairie grasses, or behind scattered trees and dust 
clouds. If the result exceeds the highest passive 
Perception among the pursuers, that longhorn escapes 
the chase and cannot be recovered.  
 The jackalweres abandon the chase once they have 
rendered a longhorn unconscious using their Sleep 
Gaze. Once a longhorn collapses, the jackalweres close 
in for the slaughter. 
Running the Chase 
A chase participant can take the Dash action a number 
of times equal to 3 plus its Constitution modifier 
(minimum of once). Each additional Dash action it takes 
during the chase requires the creature to succeed on a 
DC 10 Constitution saving throw at the end of its turn or 
gain 1 Exhaustion level. A participant drops out of the 
chase if its Speed is 0. 
 During the chase, participants can make attacks, cast 
spells at creatures within range, or perform the 
Influence action if adjacent to a longhorn. A character 
can attempt to calm and control a longhorn by using the 
Influence action to make a Wisdom (Animal Handling) 
check against DC 15. On a success, the longhorn settles 
down and stops resisting. On a failure, the creature 
remains agitated and continues trying to flee. If a 
longhorn is restrained or otherwise unable to flee, it 
drops out of the chase.

Wilderness Complications 
Once the stampede begins, the longhorns tear across 
the open prairie in a blind panic, kicking up dust and 
mowing through brush. The herd moves as one, hooves 
pounding like thunder, but any creature close behind 
quickly realizes that the plains are far from smooth. Dry 
creek beds, gnarled trees, and windblown hazards turn 
the chase into a series of unpredictable challenges. 
 At the end of each participant’s turn, roll 1d12. On a 
result of 1–6, roll or choose a result from the 
Wilderness Chase Complications table. On a 7–12, no 
complication occurs.  
TABLE 1.1 WILDERNESS COMPLICATIONS 
1d12 Complication 
1 You clearly hear someone singing loudly from a 
nearby bush. Make a DC 10 Wisdom saving throw 
or be momentarily distracted and lose 10 feet of 
movement. The voice comes from a singing bush 
(awakened shrub). 
2 You smack into an invisible fence post left behind 
by the Harpells. Make a DC 13 Dexterity saving 
throw or take 1d6 bludgeoning damage and lose 
10 feet of movement.  
3 Make a DC 10 Constitution saving throw. On a 
failed save, blowing dust, dirt, or pollen causes 
you to have the Blinded condition until the end of 
your turn. While you are blinded in this way, your 
Speed is halved. 
4 A sudden drop catches you by surprise. Make a DC 
10 Dexterity saving throw to navigate the 
impediment. On a failed save, you fall 10 feet. 
5 Your path takes you through a thicket of prairie 
cactus. Make a DC 15 Dexterity saving throw or 
use 10 feet of movement (your choice) to carefully 
avoid the spines. On a failed save, you take 1d10 
piercing damage as the cactus thorns rake your 
arms and legs. 
6 You trip over a single dirty left boot sitting upright 
in the grass. Make a DC 10 Dexterity saving throw 
or fall Prone. If you pick it up, it doesn’t fit anyone.  
7-12 There is no complication 
 
Ending the Chase 
The chase ends when all the longhorns have been 
calmed or restrained, or after 30 minutes of play have 
passed. Any cattle still running free by then are 
considered lost to the plains. If the characters interfered 
with the jackalwere hunt, they acquire the Story 
Award: Gogan Brothers Gang Feud.  
Development 
Unless the characters left someone to guard the cart, 
they return to an unfortunate surprise. During the 
chaos, Honey apparently wandered off, crossing a 
shallow dry ravine in pursuit of a patch of prickly pear 
growing on the far side. The mule made it across just 
fine. The cart didn’t. Its axle cracked on the descent, and 
the already top-heavy, ugly-as-sin statue tipped over 
and smashed against the rail. Now, the shattered 
remains lie in jagged pieces across the cart bed. Honey 
stands beside it, cheerfully munching cactus pads with 
total disinterest. 
Reward 
If the characters return with at least half as many 
longhorns as there are members in their party 
(minimum 1), Lee, grateful for their help, rummages 
through her dusty pack and offers them a weathered 
leather pouch containing 20 gold pieces. If any of the 
lassos remain intact, Lee tells the characters they can 
keep them. 
 If the characters fail to bring back any longhorns, or 
fewer than the required amount, Lee is still thankful. 
With a resigned sigh, she mutters about how she’s likely 
to lose her job and prepares for the long ride back to 
Zelorrgosz’s ranch.  
Encounter B. Ol’ Stinkybutt 
This encounter occurs after Lee, now mounted on her 
horse, departs with any longhorns the characters 
managed to wrangle. Read aloud or paraphrase the 
following: 
A shifting shadow glides over the tall grass. Moments later, 
the copper dragon swoops down and lands nearby, kicking 
up grass and dirt. The dragon bows low and calls out, 
"That was some mighty fine wrangling. You handled that 
better than I figured!" 
Ol’ Stinkybutt (young copper dragon) introduces 
themself as Nuragha. The copper dragon is talkative and 
shows all the signs of teenage immaturity. Slung 
beneath one foreleg by a makeshift leather strap is a 
wooden barrel of Mira’s Creamy Condiment Co., its 
sides stained with streaks of oily white goo and sloshing 
ominously with spoiled mayonnaise, the dragon’s fuel 
for his infamous aerial bombardments.  
THE JOKE THAT WENT TOO FAR 
Originally from the south, Nuragha is a young copper dragon 
who claimed a patch of rugged land near the southern Crag 
Mountains. One quiet afternoon as Nuragha lounged on a 
sunbaked ridge, watching a group of ranch hands mend a 
fence. One of the ranchers paused to tip back a drink when a 
passing bird delivered an accidental aerial bombardment right 
on his hat. The ranch hand yelped, slipped, and landed face-
first in a cow patty. Nuragha roared with laughter. It was the 
funniest thing the young dragon had ever seen. If a bird could 
bring such joy by accident, imagine what a dragon could do on 
purpose. From that day on, Nuragha took to the skies, 
dropping “gifts” on Longsaddle’s ranchers as part of his grand 
comedy. In his mind, it was a layered joke. Manure helps 
crops grow, right? Surely they’d be grateful. But fewer 
ranchers wave hello these days, and more wave pitchforks. 
Still, Nuragha remains convinced they'll come around 
eventually.

NURAGHA “OL’ STINKYBUTT” 
Chaotic Good Copper Dragon (he/him) prankster  
A lanky young copper dragon the size of a warhorse with an 
oversized grin and a tendency to talk with their manta-like 
wings.  
What They Want. Nuragha wants to make people laugh, 
lighten their burdens, and bring joy to the world through 
elaborate pranks and spontaneous humor. Unfortunately, 
they often fail to understand the line between harmless fun 
and genuine trouble.  
Dragon Teen. Nuragha is loud, attention seeking, and 
convinced they are much funnier than they actually are. 
After explaining their “comedy,” Ol’ Stinkybutt looks at 
the characters expectantly, hoping for laughter or 
applause. When none is immediately forthcoming, the 
dragon frowns, tilts their head, and asks with genuine 
confusion, “Wait. You mean folks ain’t enjoying this? 
That one had visual flair! Heck! I gave them free manure 
for their farm! Farmers love manure!” 
 The dragon listens to the characters, but interrupts 
often, defends their “art,” and insists that humor is 
subjective. Still, Ol’ Stinkybutt is not hostile, just 
clueless. Their starting attitude is Indifferent.  
Development 
The characters may resolve the situation by making a 
group check using one of the following Influence actions 
against a DC 15: 
• A character can make a Charisma (Persuasion) check 
to explain why folks are angry or offer suggestions 
for more acceptable pranks. 
• A character can make a Charisma (Deception) check 
to trick Ol’ Stinkybutt by exaggerating the severity of 
the ranchers’ reaction, pretending the Harpells are 
preparing magical retaliation, or claiming the 
longhorn statue is a tribute from a secret admirer. 
• A character can attempt to scold Ol’ Stinkybutt and 
point out that their pranks have harmed people. They 
can even point out that their dung isn’t even fit for 
fertilizer. This requires a Charisma (Intimidation) 
check. 
If the characters brought the giant longhorn statue or 
decide to use another appropriate gift, Ol’ Stinkybutt is 
immediately enthralled by the gift. The characters gain 
Advantage on their Charisma-related skill checks. 
 Success convinces the copper dragon to try a new 
approach and stop the dung-bombing. Eventually, they 
sigh, stretches their wings, and nods in agreement. Read 
or paraphrase the following:  
"Fine. Perhaps the joke got a little out of hand. I believed 
folks would laugh. But if all I am doing is making people 
mad, then maybe it is time to rethink my approach."  
 
 
Copper Plopper 
If the characters fail their group check, Ol’ Stinkybutt 
becomes defensive and flies off in a huff. As the dragon 
rises into the sky, he circles once, then lines up a parting 
shot. The characters have one round to act before the 
dragon prepares to bombard them. This is an 
improvised scene. If their preparations make sense and 
they succeed on a relevant check against DC 12, they 
gain Advantage on their upcoming Dexterity saving 
throw against the dragon’s parting gift. 
 With theatrical flair, and zero shame, the dragon 
bombards a 20-foot-radius area centered on as many 
characters as possible. Each creature in the area must 
succeed on a DC 15 Dexterity saving throw or be 
thoroughly splattered, taking 1d6 psychic damage from 
the shame. The foul stench lingers on the character for 
the next 24 hours. A prestidigitation cantrip or a very 
good bath can remove the stench. The copper dragon 
then departs for the Crags, choosing to lay low until the 
folk of Longsaddle have had time to cool off.  
 If the characters attack Ol’ Stinkybutt, the copper 
dragon shrieks in surprise, flares their wings, and 
immediately takes flight. The dragon wants no part of a 
serious fight, but they refuse to leave without leaving 
their mark. Before fleeing, the dragon banks hard, loops 
around, and delivers their signature parting prank. 
Afterwards, Ol’ Stinkybutt vanishes into the skies and 
retreats deep into the Crags. 
 A failure outcome results in the characters receiving 
the Story Award: Watch the Skies.  
Treasure 
Ol’ Stinkybutt remarks that the party has a lot of guts 
for standing up to a dragon and says they deserve 
something shiny for their courage. The copper dragon 
then begins to cough and gag like a cat with a hairball. 
After several dramatic heaves, they regurgitate a 
weathered six-string guitar glistening with dragon spit. 
Ol’ Stinkybutt assures the characters it is one of their 
favorites. The copper dragon then departs for the Crags 
(taking their gift if there is one).  
 On the ground is the guitar, an Instrument of Illusions 
imbued with the Blissful quirk.  
Conclusion 
After dealing with the copper dragon, whether through 
diplomacy or disaster, the characters can return to 
Longsaddle. People are surprised to see them alive. 
Word spreads quickly, and by sundown, the locals 
gather at the Horn and Hoof tavern to celebrate the 
brave souls who stood face-to-face with Ol’ Stinkybutt. 
 True to their word, the Harpells hands over a Shield 
of Expression imbued with the Metamorphic quirk to 
each of the characters as their reward. The face on the 
shield is a sculpted likeness of the dark elf ranger, Drizzt 
Do’Urden, created during a brief attempt to profit off 
the dark elf’s widespread fame. When the shields failed 
to sell, they were tucked away in the Harpell’s family 
vaults.
