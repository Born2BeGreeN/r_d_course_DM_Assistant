KETTLEPOT’S SHOP OF ODDITIES  
THE MYSTERY SURROUNDING THE DISAPPEARANCE  
 OF A LOCAL SHOPKEEPER 
 
Treyos Kettlepot was a child when he stowed away on a ship owned by dwarven traders from 
Waterdeep.  Once arriving in Chult, Kettlepot spent what remained of his childhood living as a 
street urchin in Crelt, a town lying along the southern coastline of the island.  At the age of 
fifteen, Kettlepot had a chance meeting with Kralt Freel, a local mage and a collector of 
oddities from the numerous shipwrecks which dotted the beachfront and rocky bluffs of Wild 
Coast.  Freel became a father to Treyos, teaching him the business and eventually helping 
Kettlepot to become a local proprietor himself.  Until recently, Kettlepot witnessed a modest 
amount of success.  He was locally known for providing a fair price in the purchase and trade 
of unique and magical artifacts.  The week prior to the Festival of Thard, Kettlepot’s shop 
remained closed.  Because there was no explanation and no forewarning, customers assumed 
that the old human had taken the opportunity to visit friends from Mezro.  Two weeks have 
past; residents of Crelt are becoming worried—many wonder what has happened.  Where is 
he? And, what is to become of Kettlepot’s Shop of Oddities?   
This adventure is best played with a group of 2-4 characters of  
level 3-6.

INTRODUCTION  
KETTLEPOT’S SHOP OF ODDITIES is an adventure for characters 
between a third and sixth level of play.  The Dungeon Master (DM) 
may choose to connect this short module with the TOMB OF 
ANNIHILATION story arc; however, players from any location within 
the island continent of CHULT may travel to Crelt to solve the 
mysterious disappearance of Treyos Kettlepot.   
The adventure is broken into three parts.  Adventurers will visit the 
town of Crelt and take in its unique and magical qualities.  Next, they 
will visit Kettlepot’s shop to determine what may have happened to 
the old shopkeeper.  Finally, and with the aid of a variety of clues 
and unnatural experiences, the characters will discover Treyos’s fate 
and what evil stands behind his disappearance.    
THE TOWN OF CRELT Crelt does not appear in the lore of The Forgotten Realms.  It was 
established in 1137 DR by a group of explorers and traders who 
wished to organize a safe haven and trade cooperative along the 
southern coast of the peninsula.  In its early years, the town and its 
residents experienced a trade boom as large seaport communities 
like Waterdeep became aware of region’s natural resources and the 
growing markets of its rural towns and cities.  This economic boom 
reached its height in 1389 DR when a major trade guild in 
Waterdeep identified the coastal towns and harbor cities of Chult as 
their primary foreign market.  As Waterdeep guilds grew and 
prospered, so did the demand for the expansion of its trade 
networks beyond the Sword Coast.  At its height, Crelt was home to 
2,800 residents, many traveling to the area from the Sword Coast 
and the Western Heartlands.  Those arriving in this coastal 
community included militia members and mercenaries, academics 
and arcane researchers, and maritime traders.  The town has 
continued to prosper; however, the competing factions and corrupt 
trade practices of communities to the north and west have spread to 
this island continent.  
The DM may begin by reading the following: 
 
The Dungeon Master (DM) may wish to allow his or her players to 
reflect on what brought them to this community—it may be as 
simple as needing rations and goods for their exploration of Chult.  
Or, it may be the rumors of Crelt’s exotic wares and magical streets. 
If the characters need more information about the town and the 
current dangers, read the following text: 
 
TOWN NPCS 
Crelt is a diverse town.  Being so close to the southern coastline of 
the continent, this community is not only filled with the many races 
of the area, but it includes traders, explorers, and academics from 
the Sword Coast.  Some residents—many living in the southern part 
of the town—are emigres from as far north as Neverwinter.    
The two important NPCs are (1) Kettlepot’s longtime friend Krel 
Trowing, a dwarf from Port Nyanzaru, and (2) a human street urchin 
who goes by the name of Sped.  Both are aware of Kettlepot’s 
recent disappearance—they will seek the help of anyone who does 
not appear to be from Crelt.  The DM may use Krel or Sped to move 
the story forward.  Sped spends his days running through the market 
looking for wealthy travelers.  He is known to be an avid pickpocket 
of foreign visitors.  It will require a DC 17 to persuade Krel or Sped to 
join the party; however, if convinced, they will lead the travelers to 
any place they wish to go.  Krel and Sped will emphasize the 
importance of visiting Kettlepot’s shop. 
The DM may choose to introduce Krel as the vender crying out for 
customers to purchase his dragon fruit (see the above-listed 
reading), or someone with a passive perception of 17 or less may 
be pickpocketed by Sped—he will get caught. 
KREL TROWING (NPC) 
Krel Trowing was born to Dwarven parents living under the Peaks of 
Flame.  His early years were spent working alongside his father in 
local mines.  It is in this environment that Krel learned two 
important lessons—life is filled with trials and tribulation, and if one 
is to make his life above ground, he must seek his wealth in honest 
trade.  It is for these reasons that Trowing has not sought 
membership among any of the local guilds, choosing instead to seek 
his fortune as an entrepreneur.   
Krel is not much of a fighter; however, he is master of making and 
closing “the deal.”  He imports fruit and fine metalwork from 
Calimshan in West Faerûn.  His contracts require him to coordinate 
overland transports from harbor towns in the north. 
 
You and your fellow adventurers have entered the bustling town of 
Crelt.  As the sun rises over the edge of a nearby mountain range, 
shopkeepers, bankers, and street merchants open their businesses.   
Unlike your previous experiences in rural parts of the continent, the 
residents of this community hardly notice your group.   
The only attention that you receive comes from the numerous 
venders that recognize travelers with swollen coin purses and 
exotic weapons. 
 
 
As you take in the busy streets, you hear patrons whispering 
about a local shopkeeper who has not been seen for the last two 
weeks.  Nearby venders scoff at the concern of their young 
patrons, remarking that “old Kettlepot is probably visiting his 
magic-user friends in Mezro… he’ll be back.” 
 
As your attention returns to the busy street, you hear a vender cry 
out.  “Goods for sale!  The finest dragon fruit in all of Chult!  Don’t 
pass up your chance.  The fruit is going fast!”

SPED THE STREET URCHIN (NPC) 
Sped is an annoying scrap.  His origins are unknown; however, his 
ability with slight-of-hand and his knowledge of gambling lead many 
to believe that he was trained by Crelt’s thieves guild—a group of 
local fire knives who call themselves “The Underlings.”   
If asked, Sped will deny his wrongdoing, choosing to describe himself 
as a “right-young lad, seeking fame and fortune in Chult’s most 
beautiful town!” 
As the story unfolds, the adventurers will realize that Sped has a soft 
place in his heart for Kettlepot.  Many of the merchants who had 
shops near Treyos’s establishment, would describe the little thief as 
Kettlepot’s personal project.   
“He loved that filthy little thief—I don’t think Sped would steal 
from Treyos if his life depended on it.”   
In fact, many believe that Kettlepot was getting ready to retire, and 
as a one-time street urchin himself, Kettlepot had a soft place in his 
heart for Sped.  It is commonly understood that Sped would be 
Kettlepot’s heir—he stands to inherit Treyos’s shop and all of its 
magical goods. 
  
ADVENTURING CONTEXT AND THE 
STORY HOOK 
When the adventurers arrive in Crelt they will be presented with 
men, women, and children running from shop to shop gathering 
food and goods for daily living.  Most patrons will be too busy to 
talk, but the vendors will always be willing to answer a question or 
sell their wares to foreign travelers.     
If the adventurers attempt to stop and interrogate a resident of the 
town (DC 13 to strike up a conversation) they will quickly find out 
that even though Crelt is a tightly knit community, the design and 
layout of its neighborhoods result in the sharing of very little 
personal information.  Patrons will direct questions to local 
shopkeepers and street vendors if asked about Kettlepot.

A trail of smoke is visible from the rooftop of Kettlepot’s shop.  The 
smoke is white and does not appear to be the result of a large fire.  
In fact, players with a passive perception score of 12 or higher will 
surmise that this smoke is from a source that is nearly 
extinguished—whatever is associated with the smoke is smoldering. 
Once the note is found, the party will hear a breathing noise (like a 
sniffing dog) coming from the bottom of the door.  As they take note 
of this sound, the breathing will stop and a low growl will be heard.  
An insight or perception (wisdom) check (DC 13) will reveal that this 
is a dog.  If Krel or Sped is present, either NPC will mention that 
Kettlepot had a dog (Gary) who was only trusting of people that he 
knows—especially those who had the foresight to provide him with 
a treat.  (This is the first of several clues that will help solve the 
mystery of the disappearance.)  
 
ENTERING THE SHOP  
The ornate door is locked.  A set of thief’s tools (DC 10) may be used 
to unlock the door.  (The door can be physically forced open with a 
strength check—DC 17.)  Once the door is breached, players will 
notice that the shop is dark.  Prior to gaining entry to the shop, 
players may wish to gain insight into the condition of the shop by 
attempting to look through the stain glass panels.  These panels are 
opaque—little, if any, information will be gained from studying the 
windows.  (If a player attempts to remove a pane of glass, only a 
dark front room will be visible.  There will be an odor of burned 
parchment that will be noticeable.) 
At this point in the adventure, the DM should provide players with 
clue cards, or challenge the players to begin to take notes and map 
their surroundings.  The DM will NOT provide a map or written clues 
for the adventurers. 
Upon opening the door, the players will notice an odor of burning or 
burned parchment.  Also, as ambient light from the street enters the 
doorway will players notice a dog staring back at them—he will 
appear out of breath and hostile. The DM will read the below-listed 
description. 
 
THE SHOP 
The front room of the shop shows signs of heavy foot traffic.  It 
contains a sales counter and chairs.  The walls of the store are 
covered in shelves.  Each shelf has bottles and objects that seem 
exotic in nature, many show evidence of exposure to saltwater.  The 
room is twenty feet across and is twenty feet deep.  The room is 
dark; however, the adventurers will note that light would have been 
provided by crude oil lamps and the stain glass windows of the front 
facade.  Unlit lamps line the walls, they are situated just above each 
shelving unit.   
The contents of the room seem well organized.  Objects are 
categorized and shelved by effect; each shelving unit is marked in 
common and dwarvish.  The markings across the stone walls of the 
shop include the follow descriptions: illusions, healers, star charts 
and lenses, spell books, folklore, cultural artifacts, insects and 
birds, ship logs, weeds and herbs, fire starters, and bones and 
mummy dust.  The below-listed chart will help to randomize a 
player’s study of each shelving unit.  Each shelf will contain up to 
eight objects.  The listed effect associated with objects will be 
connected to the area studied.  These areas coincide to the 
descriptions listed above.  The DM should study these categories 
prior to game play and limit the amount of investigation made by 
party members.  This portion of the adventure is not meant to be a
